# Awareness Stages Checklist

Use this diagnostic before writing or auditing a headline. The goal is to locate the prospect's current awareness point, not to force the market into a preferred copy angle.

## Inputs To Collect

- Product name and category.
- Product performance: what the product does for the prospect.
- Mass desire or need the product satisfies.
- Evidence that the market knows or does not know the product.
- Evidence that the market wants, doubts, resists, ignores, or feels ashamed of the desire or need.
- Evidence of prior advertising in the category and any stale claims.

## Diagnostic Flow

### 1. Is the prospect already seeking this product?

- Does he know the product by name?
- Does he know what it does?
- Does he already want it?
- Is the main missing trigger a price, discount, store, coupon, delivery, gift, or availability reason?

**If yes:** use a most-aware headline strategy.

### 2. Does the prospect know the product but not want it enough?

- Does he recognize the product or brand?
- Does he know the product is linked to a public desire?
- Is he unconvinced that it works well enough?
- Is he unaware of a new improvement, proof point, mechanism, use case, or limitation removed?
- Is the product being compared against similar products that claim the same desire?

**If yes:** use a product-aware headline strategy.

### 3. Does the prospect want the result but not know this product exists?

- Does he immediately recognize the desirability of the result?
- Is the desire present but still ill-defined or uncrystallized?
- Would naming the desire or solution make the prospect recognize himself?
- Does the copy need to prove that the solution can be accomplished?
- Does the product's mechanism need to be introduced after the desire is named?

**If yes:** use a solution-aware headline strategy.

### 4. Does the prospect recognize the problem but not the product link?

- Can he name the need or problem immediately?
- Does he feel the need before he understands your product?
- Would a direct statement of the problem stop him?
- Would a problem-plus-solution promise be stronger?
- Is the strongest angle removal of a limitation, avoidance of unpleasant work, prevention, or relief?
- Is the problem emotionally broad enough that one sharpened word or image is needed?

**If yes:** use a problem-aware headline strategy.

### 5. Is the prospect unaware, resistant, or unable to admit the desire?

- Is he not aware of the desire or need?
- Would he deny the desire or problem if stated bluntly?
- Is the issue too general, secret, embarrassing, or unacceptable for a direct headline?
- Has product name, function, desire, need, or price stopped selling?
- Has the brand slipped out of public acceptance or become old-fashioned?
- Does the ad need to call the market together through identity, emotion, dream, fear, resentment, or projected result?

**If yes:** use a completely-unaware headline strategy.

## Stage Selection Tests

| Test | If true | Likely strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Price or offer alone can move the buyer | The prospect already wants the product | Most aware |
| Product name has value but needs a sharper reason | The prospect knows the product | Product-aware |
| The result is attractive but the product is unknown | The desire needs to be crystallized | Solution-aware |
| The pain is recognized before the product is | The need must be dramatized | Problem-aware |
| Direct mention creates indifference, denial, or rejection | Identification must come first | Completely unaware |

## Headline Fit Check

Before approving a headline, ask:

- Does it begin at the prospect's current awareness point?
- Does it assume knowledge the prospect does not yet have?
- Does it explain something the prospect already knows?
- Does it mention product, price, function, or desire too early?
- Does it sell the next sentence instead of trying to carry the whole sale?
- Does it select logical prospects and repel irrelevant readers?
- If the market is unaware, can the prospect identify with the headline before seeing the product?

## Common Misdiagnoses

- Treating indifference as a price problem.
- Treating resistance as a proof problem.
- Treating an unknown product as a known brand.
- Treating a hidden or shameful need as if the prospect will accept a blunt statement.
- Treating a sophistication problem as an awareness problem: if prospects know the desire and product category but have heard too many similar claims, use sophistication-stage guidance.
- Treating stale style as stale strategy: update execution, but keep the awareness match.
