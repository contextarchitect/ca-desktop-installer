# Awareness Stages Examples

Schwartz examples showing how headline strategy changes with the prospect's State of Awareness. Examples are paraphrased or lightly cleaned for OCR artifacts while preserving the original products, scenarios, and structural beats.

### Example 1: Revere Zomar Lens Electric Eye Camera - most aware

> The ad can say, in effect: Revere Zomar Lens, Electric Eye Camera, formerly $149.50, now only $119.95. The remaining copy only needs to summarize selling points, provide a store or coupon, and close.

**Principle illustrated:** A fully aware prospect already knows the product and wants it; price or availability can be the main news.

**Relevance tag:** funnel-copy, email-copy, headlines-universal

### Example 2: Steinway, Joy, Toni, Clairol, Dole, Woodbury - product-aware desire reinforcement

> Schwartz groups product-aware reinforcement under association and sensory sharpening: Steinway as the instrument of immortals, Joy as the costliest perfume, Toni as the permanent that makes twins distinguishable, Clairol as natural hair coloring, Dole as just-picked taste, and Woodbury as skin one loves to touch.

**Principle illustrated:** When the product is known, the headline can renew desire by attaching the product to status, sensory immediacy, beauty, taste, touch, or social proof.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, ad-concepts, headlines-universal

### Example 3: Rolls Royce and Zippo - product-aware sharpening of product image

> The Rolls Royce headline makes quietness tangible by saying that at 60 miles an hour the electric clock is the loudest noise. The Zippo headline dramatizes reliability through the story of a lighter that worked after being taken from a fish.

**Principle illustrated:** Product-aware copy can sharpen the prospect's image of how the product satisfies desire by dramatizing the product or its mechanism.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, ad-concepts, headlines-universal

### Example 4: Hertz, Coca Cola, Lux, Mono, Salada, Hoover, Gaines, Lewyt, Zenith - product-aware superiority tasks

> Schwartz shows product-aware headlines extending usage ("Hertz is always nearby," "Thirst knows no season"), adding proof (Lux with screen stars, Mono paper cup, Salada in Boston), announcing new mechanisms (Hoover washing floors and vacuuming scrub water, Gaines making its own gravy), and removing limits (Lewyt dusty odors, Zenith inconspicuous hearing aid).

**Principle illustrated:** The known product needs a sharper superiority claim: broader use, stronger proof, a new mechanism, or removal of an old limitation.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, ad-concepts, funnel-copy, headlines-universal

### Example 5: Lucky Strike, whiter wash, friends, quitting work, doctors, spark plugs, 17,000 blooms - solution-aware desire crystallization

> Schwartz cites headlines that give form to an amorphous desire: "Light a Lucky..." for avoiding sweets, "Who else wants a whiter wash," "How to win friends and influence people," "To men who want to quit work some day," "When doctors feel rotten," "Run your car without spark plugs," and "17,000 blooms from a single plant."

**Principle illustrated:** A solution-aware market recognizes the desired result when the headline names it, sharpens it, proves it, makes it mysterious, or gives it wonder.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, ad-concepts, headlines-universal

### Example 6: Corns, maddening itch, transistor radios, hemorrhoids, ring and piston job, cavities - problem-aware need naming

> The problem-aware headline can be as bare as "Corns?" or as direct as "Stops maddening itch." It can remove limits for portable radios, shrink hemorrhoids without surgery, offer a ring and piston job in a tube, or prevent cavities before they happen.

**Principle illustrated:** When the prospect recognizes the problem, the headline may name the problem, state the solution, remove a limitation, substitute for an unpleasant task, or promise prevention.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, funnel-copy, email-copy, headlines-universal

### Example 7: English mistakes and bald-headed barber - problem-aware emotional sharpening and novelty

> "Do YOU make these mistakes in English?" sharpens a general embarrassment into a personally felt question. "How a bald-headed barber helped save my hair" restores freshness where direct hair-saving claims had already lost force.

**Principle illustrated:** A known problem can need emotional emphasis, and an overused solution can need a verbal twist to regain attention.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, ad-concepts, headlines-universal

### Example 8: International Correspondence Schools - completely unaware hidden dream

> "The University of the Night" does not begin with a course catalog or earning claim. It calls together men studying after daily labor, connects them to Lincoln's night study, and then leads to International Correspondence Schools as a school created for their circumstances.

**Principle illustrated:** For an unaware market, the headline can give words to a hidden dream before the product appears.

**Relevance tag:** ad-concepts, video-scripts, funnel-copy

### Example 9: Postum - completely unaware hidden fear

> "Why Men Crack..." revives Postum by avoiding stale health, pep, taste, and brand-name openings. The ad dramatizes driven men exhausting their reserve through stimulants, then introduces avoiding caffeine with Postum.

**Principle illustrated:** A fifth-stage headline can exploit a hidden fear when direct product benefits and brand recognition have stopped selling.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, ad-concepts, video-scripts, funnel-copy

### Example 10: Odorono - unacceptable problem through accepted image

> "Within the Curve of a Woman's Arm" begins with an accepted image of grace and daintiness, then carefully leads into underarm perspiration and odor before introducing Odorono as a physician-formulated toilet water.

**Principle illustrated:** When the direct problem would offend or be rejected, begin with an image the market can accept and lead gradually into the need.

**Relevance tag:** ad-concepts, video-scripts, funnel-copy, headlines-universal

### Example 11: Marlboro Tattoo Ad - completely unaware hidden desire

> Schwartz points to the Marlboro tattoo campaign as a visual solution for a desire that could not be stated bluntly: virility, importance, and sexual strength projected through cowboys, racing car drivers, sky divers, and similar figures.

**Principle illustrated:** Some hidden desires are better projected through image and identification than stated as verbal claims.

**Relevance tag:** ad-concepts, video-scripts

### Example 12: Television Owner's Guide - completely unaware common resentment

> Instead of promising "Save up to $100 a year on TV repairs," the ad opens with "Why haven't TV owners been told these facts" and taps resentment toward service fees before showing that owners can learn simple adjustments from the guide.

**Principle illustrated:** A common resentment can broaden the market beyond people already willing to accept the direct solution.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, ad-concepts, funnel-copy, headlines-universal

### Example 13: U.S. School of Music - completely unaware projected triumph

> "They laughed when I sat down at the piano. But when I started to play!" opens with ridicule turning into applause, then reveals that the triumph came from lessons by mail through the U.S. School of Music.

**Principle illustrated:** A fifth-stage headline can project the ultimate emotional reward and only later explain the mechanism.

**Relevance tag:** ad-concepts, video-scripts, funnel-copy, headlines-universal

### Example 14: Listerine - completely unaware rejected problem

> "Often a bridesmaid but never a bride" dramatizes the social result of halitosis before naming bad breath directly and introducing Listerine as the mouth wash and gargle solution.

**Principle illustrated:** When the direct problem is too insulting, project its consequence in a story the prospect can identify with.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, ad-concepts, video-scripts, funnel-copy

### Example 15: International Correspondence Schools and Grace - completely unaware reward instead of effort

> "Here's an extra $50, Grace" shifts attention away from study and effort toward the reward: promotion, more money, and gratitude to the person who urged home study through I.C.S.

**Principle illustrated:** If the work implied by the solution frightens prospects away, lead with the accomplishment or reward instead.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, ad-concepts, email-copy, funnel-copy
