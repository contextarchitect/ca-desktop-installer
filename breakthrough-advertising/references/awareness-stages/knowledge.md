# Awareness Stages Knowledge

Core concepts and foundational understanding for Schwartz's State of Awareness.

## Overview

State of Awareness locates the prospect in relation to the product, the product's performance, and the desire or need the product satisfies. The headline begins at that exact point of awareness and moves the prospect one sentence deeper into the ad.

Schwartz treats awareness as one of the three inputs that determine a headline: mass desire, State of Awareness, and State of Sophistication. The more aware the market is, the more direct the copy can be; the less aware the market is, the more the copy must begin with the prospect's present state of mind.

## Foundational Concepts

### The headline's real job

The headline does not have to sell the product, name the product, or carry the entire appeal. Its job is to stop the prospect and compel him to read the second sentence; every later sentence has the same job for the next sentence.

**Key points**:
- A headline can carry the whole sale only when the prospect already seeks the specific brand-name product and the offer adds a price or availability advantage.
- For every other awareness state, the headline should sell the next step in the ad, not compress the whole selling argument into one line.
- The less aware the market is, the more dangerous it is to put product, price, or performance into the headline too early.

### State of Awareness

**Definition**: The market's present knowledge about the product and the satisfaction the product performs.

Awareness asks how close the desire or need is to the surface of the prospect's consciousness, and whether the prospect connects that desire or need to a category, a product, a named brand, or a price.

**Key questions**:
- Does the prospect know the desire or need exists?
- Does he know that desire or need can be satisfied?
- Does he know the satisfaction lies in this product category?
- Does he know this particular product or brand?
- Does he already want it, or does he still need proof, sharpening, or redefinition?

### Psychological walls

Each stage is separated from the next by a psychological wall. A headline that works for one stage can fail completely in another because it starts from the wrong assumption about what the prospect already knows or accepts.

**Key points**:
- On one side of the wall is indifference; on the other is intense interest.
- A direct price headline can work for a fully aware buyer and fail when the prospect does not yet know the product can satisfy the desire.
- A once-successful headline can stop working when the market moves to a new awareness stage.

## The Five Awareness Stages

### Most aware

**Definition**: The customer knows the product, knows what it does, knows he wants it, and has not yet bought.

This is the most mechanical selling job. The prospect already has the information he needs, so the ad can lead with product name, price, offer, store, coupon, delivery, or another immediate buying reason.

**Headline starting point**:
- Product name
- Bargain price or special offer
- Availability, store, coupon, free gift, or delivery advantage

### Product-aware

**Definition**: The prospect knows the product, but does not yet want it enough because he is not fully aware of what it does, is not convinced how well it does it, or has not been told how much better it now does it.

This is the largest field of ordinary advertising. The product or brand is known, has attached itself to a public desire, and has shown that it can satisfy that desire; the headline must point out superiority.

**Headline starting point**:
- Product name in the headline or a logo of equal prominence
- A superiority claim that reinforces, sharpens, extends, proves, mechanizes, removes limitations, or changes the product image

### Solution-aware

**Definition**: The prospect knows or immediately recognizes that he wants what the product does, but does not yet know there is a product that will do it for him.

The copy writer must crystallize an amorphous desire and its solution so clearly that the prospect recognizes the desire at a glance. This stage demands analysis, intuition, and verbal creativity because the headline often gives a name to something still undefined.

**Headline starting point**:
- Name the desire and/or its solution
- Prove the solution can be accomplished
- Show that the mechanism is contained in the product

### Problem-aware

**Definition**: The prospect recognizes a need or problem immediately, but does not yet realize the connection between fulfilling that need and the product.

This is the problem-solving ad. The headline starts with the need, the solution, or both; the body dramatizes the need until the prospect feels its urgency, then presents the product as the inevitable solution.

**Headline starting point**:
- The naked problem
- The problem plus the solution
- The removal of a limitation
- A substitute for an unpleasant or expensive task
- Prevention of a future problem

### Completely unaware

**Definition**: The prospect is not aware of the desire or need, will not honestly admit it without being led into it, faces a need too general or amorphous for a direct headline, or has a secret desire or problem that cannot be verbalized directly.

This is the hardest awareness state. The headline cannot begin with price, product, function, or stated desire; it must begin with the market itself by echoing an emotion, attitude, hidden dream, hidden fear, resentment, protest, or identification point.

**Headline starting point**:
- An identification headline
- A universally accepted image that leads toward an unacceptable problem
- A hidden dream, hidden fear, common resentment, or projected triumph
- A first paragraph that continues the identification and begins building awareness

## How It Relates To

- **Mass desire**: Awareness does not create desire; it determines how directly the ad can name and channel the desire.
- **State of Sophistication**: Awareness asks what the prospect knows about your product and its satisfaction; sophistication asks how many similar claims or products the prospect has already seen.
- **Headlines**: The headline starts at the market's current awareness point and sells the next sentence.
- **Creative planning**: Awareness diagnosis comes before writing because it controls the opening strategy, proof burden, and amount of explanation required.

## Common Misconceptions

- **Myth**: A headline should always name the product.
  **Reality**: Product naming helps only when the prospect already knows the product or the brand carries useful meaning.

- **Myth**: A direct promise is always stronger than an indirect opening.
  **Reality**: Direct promises fail when the market is indifferent, resistant, ashamed, or unaware of the need.

- **Myth**: A successful headline can be reused indefinitely.
  **Reality**: Markets move. A headline can become obsolete after it pushes the market into a new awareness state.

- **Myth**: Style is strategy.
  **Reality**: Styles age; the underlying strategy of matching the prospect's awareness does not.

## Quick Reference

| Awareness level | What the headline can safely lead with |
|---|---|
| Most aware | Product, price, offer, buying convenience |
| Product-aware | Product plus a sharper superiority claim |
| Solution-aware | Desire or solution, then proof and mechanism |
| Problem-aware | Need, problem, limitation, prevention, or relief |
| Completely unaware | Identification with the market's hidden state of mind |
