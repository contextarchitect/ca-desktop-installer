# Awareness Stages Rules

Use these rules to match the headline and opening strategy to the prospect's present State of Awareness.

## Core Rules

### 1. Start where the market is now

Do not begin where you wish the prospect were. Begin at the prospect's current knowledge of the desire, the solution, the product category, the specific product, and the offer.

- The more aware the market, the less the headline needs to explain.
- The less aware the market, the more the headline must begin with identification and lead gradually toward product.
- A headline that works at one stage can fail at another stage because it assumes too much or too little.

### 2. Use the headline to sell the next sentence

Except for the fully aware bargain buyer, do not force the headline to do the whole selling job.

- Stop the prospect.
- Make him read the second sentence.
- Let each later sentence perform the same job for the sentence after it.
- Reserve the full selling argument for the body copy, where proof, visualization, and mechanism can work.

### 3. Most-aware rule: lead with the buying reason

When the prospect already knows the product, knows what it does, and wants it, make the headline mechanical and direct.

- State the product or brand.
- Add price, price reduction, gift, store proximity, delivery, coupon, or other buying trigger.
- Summarize the most desirable selling points quickly.
- Close with store, coupon, order path, or deadline.

### 4. Product-aware rule: name the product and prove superiority

When the product is known but desire is not yet strong enough, display the product name or logo and use the headline to sharpen its superiority.

Use one of Schwartz's seven headline tasks:

1. Reinforce desire for the product through association, sensory sharpening, or illustration.
2. Sharpen the image of the way the product satisfies the desire.
3. Extend the image of where and when the product satisfies the desire.
4. Add new proof, details, or documentation of how well the product satisfies the desire.
5. Announce a new mechanism that helps the product satisfy the desire better.
6. Announce a new mechanism that eliminates former limitations.
7. Change the image or mechanism to remove the product from direct competition.

The body copy should elaborate the superiority with visualization, documentation, and mechanization.

### 5. Solution-aware rule: crystallize the desire and solution

When the prospect wants the result but does not know the product exists, make the headline name the desire and/or solution.

- Pinpoint the ill-defined desire spreading through the market.
- Crystallize the desire sharply enough that the prospect recognizes it at a glance.
- Prove the solution can be accomplished.
- Show that the mechanism of accomplishment is contained in the product.
- Use verbal creativity to name the still-undefined feeling, hope, desire, or fear.

### 6. Problem-aware rule: name and dramatize the need

When the prospect recognizes the problem but not the product connection, begin with the need, the solution, or both.

- Use the naked problem when the problem is already vivid.
- State problem and solution when immediate relief is the strongest entry.
- Promise removal of a previous limitation when that limitation blocks belief.
- Offer a substitute for an unpleasant or expensive task.
- Promise prevention when the future problem is more motivating than current discomfort.
- If the problem is broad but emotionally uncertain, sharpen one word or image.
- If a direct solution has lost freshness, restore novelty with a verbal twist.

### 7. Completely-unaware rule: eliminate product, price, function, and stated desire

When the market is indifferent, resistant, ashamed, or unable to name the desire, do not open with the product.

- Eliminate price from headline and prime display.
- Keep the product name out of the headline; be wary of prominent logos that break the mood or disguise.
- Do not state what the product does, what desire it satisfies, or what problem it solves.
- Concentrate on the market's state of mind at this moment.
- Write an identification headline that tells prospects what they are, not what they should buy.
- Let the body progress from identification to awareness of problem or desire, then to solution, then to product.

### 8. Require identification in the unaware stage

A fifth-stage headline cannot merely be a startler, joke, curiosity line, pretty picture, or empty attention-getter.

- The prospect must identify with the headline before he can buy from it.
- The headline must be his problem, his state of mind, or his emotional situation.
- It should pick out logical prospects and reject people outside the market.
- It may use hidden dreams, hidden fears, common resentments, unacceptable problems, ultimate triumphs, or indirect projections of a result.

### 9. Watch for market movement

No market stands still. If a headline stops working, re-diagnose awareness before changing only style or wording.

- A product can slip out of public awareness or acceptance.
- Direct product, function, desire, or need claims may lose force over time.
- A stale market may require reopening through a less direct stage.
- Styles change, but the strategy of matching awareness remains.

## Quick Reference

| If the prospect... | Then the headline should... |
|---|---|
| Already wants the product | Lead with product and buying reason |
| Knows the product but lacks desire or conviction | Name product and show superiority |
| Wants the result but does not know the product | Name/crystallize desire or solution |
| Recognizes the problem but not the product link | Name and dramatize problem or need |
| Is unaware, resistant, or unable to admit the issue | Use identification before product |

## Avoid

- Leading with price when the prospect does not know or want the product.
- Leading with product name when the brand has no meaning or negative meaning.
- Leading with product function when the prospect is indifferent to that function.
- Mistaking sophistication problems for awareness problems.
- Writing a fifth-stage headline that attracts attention without selecting the market.
