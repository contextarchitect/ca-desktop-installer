# Creative Planning Examples

Schwartz examples demonstrating creative planning, research use, product personality, prevention appeals, splinter markets, and the prospect's mental dimensions.

### Example 1: Blank coffee, boat owners, and pimple ads - echo ads

> Schwartz describes copy writers who take proven headlines, remove the original product, and insert their own product or performance. The result may resemble a famous coffee line, a fact-based appeal to boat owners, or a skin-clearing promise, but it is only borrowing surface form.

**Principle illustrated:** Word substitution can produce an echo ad when it ignores the unique product-market-timing relationship.

**Relevance tag:** headlines-universal, ad-concepts

### Example 2: Motivation research questions - turning roadblocks into campaign ideas

> Schwartz gives research questions a copy writer might discover during a continuing campaign: why one woman sews most clothes at home while another uses the machine only for repairs; how to make people feel safe buying by mail order; why men buy an automatic potato peeler while women return it.

**Principle illustrated:** Motivation research is most useful when campaign limits become precise psychological questions.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, funnel-copy, email-copy

### Example 3: Cadillac - product personality summarized by quality

> Schwartz uses Cadillac as a product with many personality traits: prestige, performance, appearance, comfort, resale value, freedom from repairs, and more. He argues that quality is the trait that best summarizes the whole personality and should dominate the headline treatment.

**Principle illustrated:** Product personality should be simplified and sharpened through the one trait that carries the whole image.

**Relevance tag:** ad-concepts, headlines-universal, video-scripts

### Example 4: Decay-preventing toothpaste - prevention through children

> Schwartz explains that decay-preventing toothpaste sold well when the danger was focused on children rather than on the parent. The parent may not visualize a future dental problem for himself, but can imagine it affecting his child.

**Principle illustrated:** Prevention appeals work when future harm is transferred to someone the prospect wants to protect.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, ad-concepts, funnel-copy

### Example 5: Life insurance - prevention through wife and children

> Schwartz treats life insurance as another prevention case: the ad should not center on the prospect picturing his own death, but on the hardship imposed on his wife and children if money is not left for them.

**Principle illustrated:** The prospect may buy a preventative when the copy makes consequences for loved ones vivid and emotionally immediate.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, video-scripts, funnel-copy

### Example 6: Reducing aid - appearance market versus health market

> Schwartz notes that all women interested in losing weight do not want it for the same reason. Some seek appearance; others need weight loss for health. The same general promise can apply to both, but speed and ease may fit the first segment while safety and permanence may fit the second.

**Principle illustrated:** A market can be split by motive, and each segment may require a different mechanism and proof structure.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, ad-concepts, funnel-copy

### Example 7: Reducing aid through doctors - splinter market to avoid competition

> Schwartz describes a smaller advertiser choosing the health segment of the reducing market instead of competing for the larger appearance segment. That choice changes the headline, identification copy, mechanism, substantiation, media, and even distribution, such as using health or geriatric magazines and doctors rather than drug stores.

**Principle illustrated:** A splinter market lets a smaller campaign focus its entire strategy around a narrower, more defensible appeal.

**Relevance tag:** ad-concepts, funnel-copy, email-copy

### Example 8: Low-calorie food - desire plus identification

> Schwartz explains that a woman may buy low-calorie food not only to become thin, but also to rebuild a radiant, attractive, youthful personality. The product serves both a physical desire and an identification desire.

**Principle illustrated:** A product gains power when body copy links practical fulfillment to the self-image the prospect wants.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, video-scripts, email-copy

### Example 9: Automobile purchase - utility plus prestige

> Schwartz explains that a man may buy a car for power, speed, and transportation, but also for the prestige, success, and visible prosperity the car projects to neighbors.

**Principle illustrated:** Identification can intensify a material desire by making the product a social signal.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, ad-concepts, video-scripts
