# Creative Planning Knowledge

Core concepts and foundational understanding for Schwartz's creative planning sequence and the psychological foundation of body copy.

## Overview

Creative planning is the work that happens before the words begin: knowing the market, studying the product, finding the dominant emotional force, and locating the strongest point of entry for the headline. Once the headline has stopped the prospect, body copy sells by expanding desire, supporting identification, and building belief.

## Key Concepts

### The Three Levels of Creativity

**Definition**: Three ways copy writers try to create headline ideas, moving from shallow imitation to true analysis.

**Key points**:
- **Word-substitute technique**: swaps a product name into a proven headline and produces echo ads.
- **Formula technique**: applies headline rules mechanically; useful for expression, weak as a source of the idea.
- **Analytical approach**: uses guide-posts and questions to uncover the unique solution inside the product, market, and timing.

### Product-Market-Timing Relationship

**Definition**: The unique relationship between this product, this market, and this moment.

Schwartz treats this relationship as the only true source of the idea. A breakthrough headline cannot be borrowed from a file cabinet, because the exact product-market-timing relationship has not existed before.

**Key points**:
- The idea is inside the product and market, not inside an outside formula.
- The copy writer must push past prior successes instead of copying them.
- The final headline may be only five or ten words, but it carries most of the ad's value.

### Motivation Research

**Definition**: Research into the hidden needs, desires, taboos, market splits, and reactions that can guide copy strategy.

Motivation research can point toward the right territory and test hunches, but it is not itself a headline or theme.

**Key points**:
- Reveals desires the market may not openly state or consciously know.
- Shows desire strength, drift, taboos, and limits on expression.
- Locates market splits and gives early feedback on phraseology.
- Turns campaign failures and roadblocks into research questions.

### Product Personality

**Definition**: The complete personality a consumer assigns to a product, store, or product group.

A product may contain many traits, but one trait usually summarizes the whole personality most effectively. The headline should sharpen that dominant trait, then body copy can expand the full personality into an enlarging pyramid of persuasion.

### Prevention Through Others

**Definition**: The prevention appeal that works by making the prospect visualize future harm to people around him.

Schwartz argues that prospects resist imagining future disasters happening to themselves, but can vividly imagine danger to loved ones, family, friends, or nation. Prevention copy works best when it shifts the future consequence outward.

### Splinter Markets

**Definition**: Smaller segments of a larger market selected to avoid direct competition or make a sharper appeal.

Markets are not all of one piece. A smaller advertiser may win by selecting a sub-market based on motive, sociology, medium, or channel rather than fighting the largest competitors for the whole market.

### Creative Planning Sequence

**Definition**: The planning process that builds the theme and point of entry before the headline is written.

**Sequence**:
1. Analyze the market's breadth, depth, and emotional forces.
2. Focus those forces into one image, desire, or need.
3. Study the physical product and the functional product being sold.
4. Focus the product's satisfactions into one image, identification, or claim.
5. Combine market demand and product satisfaction into the theme.
6. Determine the market's maturity and point of entry.
7. Express the theme in a headline.
8. Use body copy to make the introduction worthwhile and build conviction.

### The Three Dimensions of the Prospect's Mind

**Definition**: The raw materials body copy works with after the headline gets attention.

| Dimension | What It Contains | Copy Task |
|-----------|------------------|-----------|
| Desires | Wants, needs, cravings, physical and material longings | Expand, sharpen, channel, and give them a goal |
| Identifications | Roles, personality traits, status, fantasies, self-image | Connect the product to the person the prospect wants to become |
| Beliefs | Opinions, prejudices, facts, attitudes, reasoning habits | Prove through the prospect's own logic and existing convictions |

## Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Echo ads | Ads that borrow the surface form of another successful ad without matching the current product-market-timing relationship. |
| Guide-posts | Analytical questions and procedures that direct thought without supplying formulas to copy. |
| Physical product | The thing delivered to the buyer. |
| Functional product | The satisfactions, performances, and outcomes the copy sells. |
| Theme | The joined promise of market desire and product satisfaction. |
| Point of entry | The place where the headline begins, based on what the prospect will notice and accept. |
| Pyramid of persuasion | Body copy that expands a summarized product personality into supporting appeals and proof. |
| Emotionalized reason | The prospect's belief system: facts, feelings, prejudices, and logic blended together. |

## How It Relates To

- **Mass desire**: Creative planning starts by identifying the emotional force already present in the market.
- **Awareness stages**: Awareness helps determine the point of entry for the headline.
- **Sophistication stages**: Market maturity affects how directly or indirectly the claim can be expressed.
- **Headlines**: The headline is the concentrated output of planning, not a substitute for planning.
- **Intensification**: The primary method for expanding desire in body copy.
- **Identification-gradualization**: The later techniques that develop self-image and belief.
- **Proof-and-focus**: The later techniques that make claims believable and concentrate conviction.

## Common Misconceptions

- **Myth**: A proven headline can be reused by swapping in a new product.
  **Reality**: That creates an echo ad unless the product, market, and timing are nearly identical.

- **Myth**: Motivation research gives the copy writer the headline.
  **Reality**: Research gives direction, questions, and feedback; the idea still must be created.

- **Myth**: The headline must sell the product.
  **Reality**: The headline's job is to stop the prospect and get him into the first paragraph.

- **Myth**: Body copy length is arbitrary.
  **Reality**: Length is determined by how much copy is needed to intensify desire, support identification, and build belief.

- **Myth**: Advertising should correct the prospect's beliefs.
  **Reality**: Advertising must start with existing beliefs and build conviction through the prospect's own logic.
