# Creative Planning Rules

Use these rules to plan an ad or campaign before drafting copy, then structure body copy around the prospect's desire, identification, and belief.

## Core Rules

### 1. Do Not Begin With a Borrowed Headline

A headline copied from another ad only works when the product, market, and timing are genuinely similar.

- Avoid word substitution as the source of the idea.
- Treat proven headline files as expression references, not idea machines.
- Check whether the borrowed structure creates an echo of another product.

### 2. Use Formulas Only After the Idea Exists

Headline formulas can strengthen verbal expression, but they cannot discover the right theme.

- Use formulas to sharpen, intensify, or clarify.
- Do not force the product into someone else's solution.
- If the formula hides the product's unique fact, discard it.

### 3. Start With Market Analysis

Before studying words, study the market.

- Measure the breadth and depth of the market.
- Identify the emotional forces that create demand.
- Focus those forces into one image, desire, or need.
- Ask which desire is strongest enough to support the whole ad.

### 4. Study the Product as Both Thing and Satisfaction

The product is not only what is delivered; it is what the prospect believes he will gain.

- List the physical product's features and operations.
- List the functional product's performances, satisfactions, and outcomes.
- Reduce the list to the single claim, image, or identification with the greatest emotional leverage.

### 5. Build the Theme From Demand Plus Satisfaction

The theme joins what the market already wants with what the product can credibly supply.

- Pair desire with satisfaction.
- Pair need with solution.
- Pair desired identification with visible expression.
- Do not write until this pairing is clear.

### 6. Locate the Point of Entry

The headline should begin where the prospect's interest and acceptance are highest.

- The point of entry may be the product, price, performance, promised satisfaction, felt need, or market identity.
- Use awareness and sophistication only to choose where to start; do not re-argue those frameworks here.
- If the market will resist the product, enter through the desire or identification instead.

### 7. Treat the Headline as an Invitation

The headline's first job is to stop the prospect and bring him into the first paragraph.

- Do not ask the headline to carry the entire sale.
- Make the opening thought strong enough to interrupt the prospect's mental activity.
- Make the body copy pay off the introduction immediately.

### 8. Let Body Copy Length Follow the Required Mental Change

The ad should be long enough to alter the prospect's mental world.

- Add enough copy to build desire to full strength.
- Add enough copy to make the product feel comfortable, flattering, and compatible with the prospect's life.
- Add enough copy to overcome existing prejudices and beliefs.
- Cut length only when those three jobs are already complete.

### 9. Intensify Desire Before Offering the Product

Your first body-copy task is to make the prospect want.

- Picture fulfillment in concrete moments.
- Make the prospect see, feel, touch, hear, or inhabit the promised world.
- Channel the desire toward the product as the goal.

### 10. Put Identification Behind the Product

Show the prospect the role, status, or personality the product helps him project.

- Connect the purchase to the group he wants to join.
- Show the people who already live in the product's world.
- Let the product serve double duty: practical satisfaction plus self-image.

### 11. Build Belief Through the Prospect's Own Logic

Do not argue with the prospect's reality. Use it as the foundation for proof.

- Start from his opinions, prejudices, accepted facts, and reasoning habits.
- Fit product information into existing belief patterns.
- Prove that the product satisfies the desire, works as claimed, is used by his kind of people, and is superior for his need.

### 12. Use Motivation Research as Direction and Feedback

Research is a compass, not finished copy.

- Use it to uncover hidden desires, taboos, and market splits.
- Use it to test worry-points, phraseology, and hunches.
- Convert campaign roadblocks into research questions.
- Keep the final transformation of facts into an idea in the copy writer's hands.

### 13. Summarize Product Personality Through One Dominant Trait

When a product has many attractive traits, choose the trait that best symbolizes the whole personality.

- Feature the summary trait in the headline or headline illustration.
- Blend secondary traits only when they strengthen the dominant trait.
- Expand the remaining appeals in body copy.

### 14. Sell Prevention by Moving the Future Disaster to Others

A prospect may resist imagining future harm to himself, but he can imagine harm to people he cares about.

- For personal prevention, consider loved ones, family, friends, or nation as the visualization target.
- Make the danger emotionally concrete without requiring the prospect to picture himself as already afflicted.
- Tie the preventative product to protection of others.

### 15. Consider a Splinter Market When the Whole Market Is Too Contested

Large markets contain sub-markets with different motives and required mechanisms.

- Segment by reason for wanting the outcome, not only by geography.
- Adjust headline, identification copy, mechanism, substantiation, media, and distribution to the chosen segment.
- If the segment succeeds, use that success later as a point of difference in the broader market.

## Guidelines

Less strict recommendations:

- Expect planning to take days, weeks, or months when the market is complex.
- Keep prior wins visible as learning, but argue with them before reusing any structure.
- Treat failed ads as diagnostic material: response limits often reveal questions the copy writer has not answered.
- Let the theme become simple before writing; complexity belongs in the research, not in the point of entry.

## Exceptions

When these rules may be relaxed:

- **Same product class, same market, same timing**: A close headline adaptation may work if the promise is also strengthened.
- **Simple, highly aware market**: Less body copy may be needed when desire, identification, and belief are already strong.
- **Existing campaign conversation**: Prior ads may supply shared assumptions, allowing a later ad to begin deeper in the argument.

## Quick Reference

| Rule | Summary |
|------|---------|
| Do not borrow the idea | Copying headlines creates echo ads. |
| Use formulas late | Formulas polish expression, not strategy. |
| Analyze market first | Find the emotional force before the claim. |
| Study product deeply | Sell satisfactions, not just features. |
| Build the theme | Join demand to satisfaction. |
| Locate entry | Start where interest and acceptance are highest. |
| Headline invites | Body copy does the selling. |
| Length follows task | Write enough to build desire, identification, and belief. |
| Research guides | MR points and tests; the copy writer creates. |
