# Headlines Checklist

Use this checklist to diagnose or strengthen a headline after the basic claim has been selected.

## Before You Start

- [ ] The dominant mass desire has been identified.
- [ ] The prospect's awareness stage has been identified.
- [ ] The market's sophistication stage has been identified.
- [ ] The headline contains the right claim, not merely an interesting phrase.
- [ ] If the market is new, a bare direct claim has been considered before adding complexity.

## 38-Way Diagnostic

Check every possible strengthening move. Do not use all of them; use the few that make the claim stronger, fresher, more believable, or more readable.

| # | Check |
|---|-------|
| 1 | Can the size of the claim be measured? |
| 2 | Can the speed of the claim be measured? |
| 3 | Can the claim be compared against alternatives? |
| 4 | Can the claim be metaphorized into a physical image? |
| 5 | Can the prospect feel, smell, touch, see, or hear the claim? |
| 6 | Can a prime example demonstrate the claim? |
| 7 | Can the claim or result be dramatized as a scene? |
| 8 | Can the claim be stated as a paradox? |
| 9 | Can a feared limitation be removed? |
| 10 | Can the claim be associated with a person or value the prospect wants to identify with? |
| 11 | Can the headline show how much work the claim does? |
| 12 | Can the claim be stated as a question? |
| 13 | Can the headline offer information about how to get the result? |
| 14 | Can authority be tied directly into the claim? |
| 15 | Can the headline show before-and-after contrast? |
| 16 | Can newness be stressed? |
| 17 | Can exclusivity be stressed? |
| 18 | Can the claim become a challenge for the reader? |
| 19 | Can the claim be phrased as a case-history quotation? |
| 20 | Can the product and what it replaces be condensed into one phrase? |
| 21 | Can the claim be symbolized by a parallel reality? |
| 22 | Can the mechanism be connected to the claim? |
| 23 | Can the mechanism contradict the way the reader expects it to work? |
| 24 | Can the need and the claim be connected in the same line? |
| 25 | Can the ad offer information the reader wants to know? |
| 26 | Can the claim or need become a case history? |
| 27 | Can the problem or need be given a name? |
| 28 | Can the headline warn about pitfalls if the product is not used? |

| 35 | Can the headline dramatize how hard it was to produce the claim? |
| 36 | Can the claim be accused of being too good? |
| 37 | Can the headline challenge the prospect's present limiting belief? |
| 38 | Can the claim be turned into a question-and-answer? |

## Chapter 14 Polish Pass

- [ ] Does the headline feed interest or curiosity that the body copy can exploit?
- [ ] If proof is included, is it short, dramatic, specific, and selling?
- [ ] Does the proof appear where the prospect is ready to want it?
- [ ] Can two images or claims be joined so their effect multiplies?
- [ ] Does the line interweave promise, image, logic, emotion, and evaluation where possible?
- [ ] Does the headline create momentum through an incomplete thought or implied answer?
- [ ] Does the rhythm match the mood: drama, sincerity, excitement, straight talk, or another chosen emotion?
- [ ] Would the emotion words feel natural to this market's tone sophistication?
- [ ] If the body copy is stronger than the headline, should a body-copy idea move to the top?

## Red Flags

- The headline is clever but not tied to the main claim.
- The headline uses a tired direct promise in a sophisticated market.
- The proof is impressive but dull.
- The question raises curiosity but does not sell.
- The tone would make this prospect feel the writer is exaggerating.
- Another brand name could be substituted without changing the headline.
