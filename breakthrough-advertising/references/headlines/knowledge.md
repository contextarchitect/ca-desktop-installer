# Headlines Knowledge

Core concepts for turning a prepared appeal into the strongest possible headline form.

## Overview

For Schwartz, the headline is the first creative search because it can decide the success or failure of the ad. The first job is strategic: determine the appeal, market desire, awareness level, and sophistication level. The second job is verbalization: state that appeal in a form that makes the claim stronger, fresher, and more likely to pull the prospect into the body copy.

## Key Concepts

### Headline Content

**Definition**: The thing the headline must say: the claim, promise, need, mechanism, or identification that best matches the prospect's present state of mind.

The content is chosen before wording. If the appeal is wrong, no verbal polish can make the headline right.

**Key points**:
- The headline should enter a desire that already exists in the prospect.
- It must match the prospect's stage of awareness.
- It must account for how many similar claims the market has already heard.

### Verbalization

**Definition**: The art of increasing the impact of a headline by the way the claim is stated.

Verbalization begins after the basic idea is known. Its job is to bind other images, measures, comparisons, mechanisms, questions, or dramatic forms into the main claim.

**Key points**:
- Strengthen the claim by making it larger, faster, more concrete, or more vivid.
- Make the claim new again by twisting, dramatizing, challenging, or reframing it.
- Pull the prospect into the ad by promising information, raising a question, or partially revealing the mechanism.

### Bare Claim

**Definition**: The simplest direct statement of the promise, such as "Lose Weight" or "Stop Corns."

The bare claim is strongest when the advertiser is first in the field or the prospect already wants exactly that product. In a crowded market, the bare claim usually needs reinforcement.

### Reinforced Claim

**Definition**: A headline claim strengthened by added images, proof, specificity, drama, authority, mechanism, or structure.

Schwartz's 38 ways are not separate strategies. They are ways to verbalize the same prepared claim so it carries more force.

### Headline as Interest and Curiosity

**Definition**: The headline starts the reader's emotional movement by feeding interest and curiosity.

Body copy then exploits what the headline aroused, leading the prospect toward mounting conviction that the product has what he wants and can deliver it.

## Relation To Awareness

Do not redefine the five awareness stages here; use `../awareness-stages/knowledge.md` as the authority. For headline work, apply them this way:

| Awareness condition | Headline implication |
|--------------------|----------------------|
| Prospect already knows the product and wants it | Use the most direct version of the product, price, offer, or claim. |
| Prospect knows the product but is not fully sold | Sharpen the product's superiority, difference, proof, or mechanism. |
| Prospect knows the solution but not this product | Connect the desired solution to the product or mechanism. |
| Prospect knows the problem but not the solution | Name or dramatize the problem, then offer information or discovery. |
| Prospect is unaware | Avoid a direct product claim; enter through identity, hidden desire, story, or symptom. |

## Relation To Sophistication

Do not redefine the five sophistication stages here; use `../sophistication-stages/knowledge.md` as the authority. For headlines:

- In a new market, the direct promise may be enough.
- In a competitive market, the same promise must be enlarged, made more specific, dramatized, mechanized, or differentiated.
- In a tired market, the headline often needs a new mechanism, a new identification, a new problem name, or a new structure rather than louder promise.
- Tone sophistication matters: emotion words that work in one medium or market can sound corny or questionable in another.

## Chapter 14 Finishing Principles

### Reinforcement

Two images or claims can combine into a new identity with more force than either has alone. Schwartz points to "We're only second" plus "We try harder" as the kind of juxtaposition that creates a stronger phrase, slogan, idea, or headline.

### Interweaving

A finished headline should carry more than bare fact when possible. Promise, image, logic, emotion, proof, and evaluation should be packed together so the line sells while it informs.

### Structure Feedback

The first headline is not sacred. If the copy story will not develop, an idea discovered in the body copy may belong at the top of the ad.

### Momentum

Headlines and subheads can create an incomplete thought that the body copy must complete. The reader moves forward to answer the question the headline has opened.

### Mood

Words and rhythm create the emotional color of the claim. Strong headlines are not only accurate; they carry the right drama, sincerity, excitement, or straightforwardness for the prospect.

## Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Claim | The promise, benefit, or assertion the headline advances. |
| Verbalization | The phrasing method that increases the impact of a known claim. |
| Mechanism | The reason or process that makes the claim believable. |
| Documentation | External proof: tests, statistics, testimonials, authorities, trends, awards, seals. |
| Reinforcement | Combining claims or images so each strengthens the other. |
| Interweaving | Blending promise, logic, emotion, and image inside the same line or sequence. |
| Momentum-builder | A phrase or incomplete statement that makes the reader continue. |
| Emotion-definer | A word that tells the reader what to feel about the image or claim. |

## Common Misconceptions

- **Myth**: A stronger headline means a bigger claim.
  **Reality**: A headline may become stronger through proof, specificity, mechanism, contrast, tone, or structure, not just larger promise.

- **Myth**: The 38 ways replace awareness and sophistication.
  **Reality**: They are verbal tools applied after the strategic appeal has been chosen.

- **Myth**: Proof in a headline is automatically convincing.
  **Reality**: Proof must be short, dramatic, specific, and placed where the prospect is ready to want it.

- **Myth**: The first headline should stay fixed while writing body copy.
  **Reality**: If the body copy reveals the true selling idea, move that idea to the top.
