# Headlines Rules

Use these rules after the market desire, awareness stage, sophistication stage, and basic claim have been chosen.

## Core Rules

### 1. Start With The Claim, Then Verbalize It

Do not begin with wordplay. First identify the strongest claim the prospect is ready to hear; then choose the verbal form that increases its force.

### 2. Use The Bare Claim Only When It Is Still Fresh

If you are first in the field, the simplest form can be the best form. In a competitive or complex market, reinforce the claim with measurement, proof, mechanism, drama, identification, or curiosity.

### 3. Make Every Added Element Serve The Claim

Every variation must bind back into the main promise. Do not add cleverness, authority, questions, or drama unless they increase desire, believability, freshness, or readership.

### 4. Use Chapter 14 Polish Before Finalizing

Check whether the headline can combine two images, interweave promise with proof or mechanism, open a curiosity gap, or carry a stronger mood through rhythm and word choice.

## Chapter 14 Finishing Rules

- **Proof must sell**: If documentation appears in or near the headline, keep it short, dramatic, specific, and emotionally charged.
- **Position increases power**: Put proof, authority, or mechanism where the reader is ready to want it, not merely where there is space.
- **Combine images deliberately**: Look for two claims or images that multiply each other when joined.
- **Pack the sentence**: Whenever possible, make the line carry promise, image, logic, emotion, and evaluation together.
- **Use structure feedback**: If body copy exposes a stronger promise, mechanism, or proof than the current headline, rebuild the headline around that discovery.
- **Read for mood**: Adjust vocabulary and rhythm to the prospect's sophistication in tone, not only product-awareness.
