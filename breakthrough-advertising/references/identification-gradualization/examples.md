# Identification + Gradualization Examples

Schwartz examples showing how products acquire role-value and how copy creates a chain of belief.

### Example 1: Food selection - role multiplication

> Schwartz notes that the average American no longer buys food only for physical satisfaction. He selects foods that make him feel modern, slim, cosmopolitan, adventurous, and sophisticated.

**Principle illustrated:** A product can attach multiple character roles to the same functional need, creating new desire angles.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, ad-concepts, funnel-copy

### Example 2: Philosophy book, Speed-Reading Course, and bookshelves - character role mastery

> The philosophy book helps the prospect appear well-read; the Speed-Reading Course speeds mastery; the bookshelf displays that mastery to others.

**Principle illustrated:** A product may help achieve a role, accelerate a role, or symbolize the role after it has been achieved.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, funnel-copy, email-copy

### Example 3: $5,000 sports car - character reinforcement beyond function

> A man who drives only on crowded parkways still pays for the sports car because its speed, gearshift, and handling make him a "sportsman" and probably a successful one.

**Principle illustrated:** Role-giving power can make an otherwise irrational purchase feel emotionally rational.

**Relevance tag:** ad-concepts, video-scripts

### Example 4: Newly-wed woman - achievement role display

> Schwartz describes the wedding ring, trousseau, new linens, furniture, and home as physical expressions of the new role "Wife."

**Principle illustrated:** Achievement roles become real to others through visible possessions.

**Relevance tag:** ad-concepts, funnel-copy

### Example 5: Marlboro - intensifying a primary image

> Marlboro took the accepted virility of cigarettes and deepened it with virile men, virile occupations, and the tattoo as a primitive symbol of daring.

**Principle illustrated:** Favorable primary images can be intensified through repeated, varied symbols of the same emotion.

**Relevance tag:** ad-concepts, video-scripts, headlines-universal

### Example 6: Chesterfield "Blow Some My Way" - bridge from unfavorable image

> Because cigarettes were accepted as a man's product, the ad kept the man in the scene but subdued him, making the woman and the romantic setting the emotional focus.

**Principle illustrated:** Do not deny an unfavorable primary image; include it, lower its intensity, and bridge to a more desirable image.

**Relevance tag:** ad-concepts, video-scripts

### Example 7: Piston rings in a sports-car shop - neutral product emotionalized

> Schwartz takes a neutral product, piston rings, and builds a world of precision, sports-car performance, expert mechanics, and successful ownership around the act of replacement.

**Principle illustrated:** Even neutral products can gain prestige when their required primary situation is dramatized to its emotional limit.

**Relevance tag:** ad-concepts, video-scripts, funnel-copy

### Example 8: TV Repair Manual - believable headline before power claim

> The tested power headline promised large repair savings and failed. The winning structure began with suspicion: "Why haven't TV owners been told these facts?"

**Principle illustrated:** A smaller but more believable first statement can outperform a larger raw promise.

**Relevance tag:** headlines-universal, funnel-copy

### Example 9: TV Repair Manual - habit of agreement

> The ad asks whether the set was purchased after spring 1947, then catalogs familiar service fees, ghosts, jumpy pictures, and repair frustrations before introducing larger claims.

**Principle illustrated:** Inclusion questions and symptom catalogs create yes-reactions that later claims inherit.

**Relevance tag:** funnel-copy, email-copy, headlines-universal

### Example 10: TV Repair Manual - delayed owner repair claim

> The ad first proves that sets can be dependable when properly cared for, then introduces the idea that the owner can make minor outside adjustments.

**Principle illustrated:** The goal conclusion should arrive only after the prospect has been prepared to accept it.

**Relevance tag:** funnel-copy, video-scripts

### Example 11: The Art of Selfishness - inclusion questions

> The opening asks the reader to answer questions about coping with the world, career frustration, and intimate inadequacy, placing the right prospect into the story.

**Principle illustrated:** Inclusion questions build immediate identification and make later recommendations feel personally relevant.

**Relevance tag:** email-copy, funnel-copy

### Example 12: Spark plug syllogism - logical inevitability

> The copy links gasoline, spark, explosion, piston movement, power, and mileage so that a larger spark appears to mean better performance by necessity.

**Principle illustrated:** Syllogistic structure makes a product claim feel shown, not merely asserted.

**Relevance tag:** funnel-copy, ad-angles, headlines-universal
