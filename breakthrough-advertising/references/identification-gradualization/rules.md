# Identification + Gradualization Rules

Use these rules to build product personality and belief architecture without contradicting what the prospect already accepts.

## Core Rules

### 1. Give Every Product Two Reasons to Buy

Offer both the functional satisfaction and the role the product lets the prospect play.

- Identify the physical want the product satisfies.
- Identify the character or achievement role attached to that satisfaction.
- Make the role a natural extension of the product, not an arbitrary overlay.

### 2. Discover Accepted Roles Before You Dramatize Them

The market decides which identifications are believable.

- List the roles the prospect already values.
- List the roles already associated with the product or category.
- Reject roles that require the prospect to make an impossible self-image jump.
- Choose the most compelling role that the product can credibly carry.

### 3. Build From the Product's Primary Image

Never contradict the accepted product image. Use it as the base.

- If the primary image is favorable, intensify it.
- If the primary image is neutral, dramatize its most emotional implication.
- If the primary image is negative, subordinate it and use it as a bridge.
- Add prestige images only when they logically emerge from the primary image.

### 4. Prefer Symbol and Situation for Character Roles

Character roles are often accepted more easily when implied.

- Use visual or verbal symbols that suggest the role.
- Avoid making a direct boast the prospect would have to defend.
- Let the prospect accept the role without a conscious test.
- Combine several symbols around one dominant emotion.

### 5. Make Achievement Roles Displayable

Achievement roles need physical symbols.

- Show the possession that makes the status visible.
- Tie the product to the prospect's next social step.
- Make the product feel like documentation of success.
- Keep the role within the prospect's believable present or near future.

### 6. Start Gradualization With an Accepted Fact

Open with something the prospect can accept instantly.

- Use a resentment, symptom, question, familiar problem, or accepted belief.
- Do not lead with the goal conclusion if it would trigger disbelief.
- Make the first statement both interesting and believable.
- Build a "habit of agreement" before asking for the larger belief.

### 7. Delay the Mechanism Until the Prospect Is Ready

Withhold the how when it would stop belief too early.

- State symptoms and desired results first if the mechanism sounds difficult.
- Keep early promises specific enough to attract and incomplete enough to pull forward.
- Introduce the mechanism as a natural continuation of accepted facts.
- Frame new claims as extensions of already accepted conclusions.

### 8. Use Belief-Carrying Structures

Structure can transfer acceptance from one claim to the next.

- Use inclusion questions to let the prospect classify himself into the story.
- Use detailed symptom identification to prove the ad is talking about him.
- Use "if...then" and similar constructions to imply conditions and logic.
- Use paragraph parallelism to make a new claim feel like part of an accepted series.
- Use logical language: "here's why," "therefore," "the reason," "because," "in case after case."

## Guidelines

Less strict recommendations:

- Add role value especially when products are functionally similar and price is not decisive.
- Let role images broaden the market by drawing in fringe emotional appeals.
- Use the physical product, components, structure, background, package, and setting as image sources.
- When testing headlines, compare raw power claims against more believable entry claims.
- Treat every ad as an architecture of reactions, not a sequence of sentences.

## Exceptions

- **Built-in prestige product**: A sports car, swimming pool, or diamond bracelet may already embody the desired identification and need less image construction.
- **Fully familiar mechanism**: If the prospect already understands and accepts the mechanism, Gradualization can move faster.
- **Urgent crisis**: A prospect frightened beyond ordinary belief may accept a larger claim sooner, but the ad still needs a coherent belief path.

## Quick Reference

| Rule | Summary |
|------|---------|
| Two reasons to buy | Sell satisfaction plus self-definition. |
| Accepted roles first | The market sets the image boundary. |
| Primary image base | Build from what the product already means. |
| Symbolize character | Suggest flattering qualities indirectly. |
| Display achievement | Turn status into visible possession. |
| Accepted fact first | Start where the prospect already says yes. |
| Delay hard mechanism | Do not introduce disbelief before preparation. |
| Structure belief | Use sequence, parallelism, and logic language. |
