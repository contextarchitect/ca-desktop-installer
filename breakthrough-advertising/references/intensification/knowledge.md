# Intensification Knowledge

Core concepts and foundational understanding for Schwartz's first breakthrough copy technique: Intensification.

## Overview

Intensification is the process of building desire by presenting a series of fresh, concrete fulfillments of the prospect's dominant desire. It does not change the desire; it sharpens, magnifies, and gives form to desire until skepticism, lethargy, and price feel less important than participation.

Schwartz treats advertising as "the literature of desire": the copy writer gives vague wants a goal, a scene, a proof-like texture, and repeated emotional weight.

## Key Concepts

### Intensification

**Definition**: The repeated presentation of new and different fulfillments for the prospect's dominant desire.

Intensification takes an indistinct want and turns it into vivid scenes of satisfaction: what the product looks like, does, proves, prevents, simplifies, and makes possible.

**Key points**:
- Reinforce one dominant desire instead of wandering into unrelated appeals.
- Vary the viewpoint every time the same promise appears again.
- Use concrete images, actions, tests, reactions, contrasts, catalogs, and guarantees.
- Stop when the next perspective feels repetitive rather than additive.

### Dominant Image

**Definition**: The one basic idea or fulfillment scene the prospect carries away from the ad.

Every additional description should make this image sharper and more emotionally charged. The reader may remember only one image, but many perspectives can build its depth.

**Key points**:
- Choose the image that best embodies the dominant desire.
- Make each supporting passage a fresh angle on that same image.
- In campaigns, compress the dominant image into a central idea and vary its presentation over time.

### Reinforcement Without Repetition

**Definition**: Restating the same promise through new settings, details, actors, or proof situations.

Schwartz warns that the writer cannot merely repeat. Once a fulfillment has been presented one way, the next presentation must change the perspective or be removed.

**Key points**:
- New setting: place the same benefit in another part of the prospect's life.
- New action: show the product doing the work instead of only describing the outcome.
- New witness: let the reader, neighbors, experts, or average users react.
- New contrast: put the old problem and new solution side by side.

### Concrete Fulfillment

**Definition**: A specific sensory or practical scene that lets the prospect experience the desired result before purchase.

The copy writer becomes "the script writer for your prospect's dreams" by showing the product's future in detail: rooms filled with roses, a car surging from a stoplight, a lure swimming while the fisherman sleeps.

**Key points**:
- Use numbers, dimensions, sequence, duration, touch, sound, color, and social reaction.
- Show what the prospect gets, what he leaves behind, and what he may be missing.
- Make price feel smaller by making the fulfilled desire feel larger.

### Compression and Campaign

**Definition**: Two adaptations for media that cannot hold a full long-copy demonstration.

When space is limited, the writer compresses images into key words or carries the intensification across a campaign. In national advertising, intensification shifts from paragraph-by-paragraph buildup inside one ad to variation across a series.

**Key points**:
- Keep the central campaign idea identifiable.
- Freshen the opening, illustration, analogy, or fact so the prospect reads again.
- Maintain continuity without forcing the same headline or lead picture until it becomes unreadable.

## The Thirteen Ways at a Glance

| # | Way | Function |
|---|-----|----------|
| 1 | First presentation of claims | Directly describe the product, appearance, or result in full detail. |
| 2 | Put claims in action | Show how the product works and produces the benefit. |
| 3 | Bring in the reader | Place the prospect inside the first day of ownership. |
| 4 | Show how to test claims | Turn the demonstration into a specific proof the reader can perform. |
| 5 | Stretch benefits in time | Show the product working over days, weeks, months, or years. |
| 6 | Bring in an audience | Add other people whose reactions renew the scene. |
| 7 | Show experts approving | Use informed astonishment to sharpen believability and desire. |
| 8 | Compare, contrast, prove superiority | Put old and new side by side so the advantage becomes obvious. |
| 9 | Picture the black side | Paint the problem or old product vividly, then supply the solution. |
| 10 | Show ease of benefit | Contrast tiny effort with large payoff. |
| 11 | Use metaphor, analogy, imagination | Dramatize fact through figurative or personified presentation. |
| 12 | Summarize before closing | Use a catalog to stack applications or desires. |
| 13 | Put the guarantee to work | Turn the guarantee into a final benefit summary. |

## Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Desire | The motivating force copy expands vertically by sharpening and magnifying. |
| Dominant desire | The main want the ad chooses to intensify. |
| Dominant image | The central fulfillment image the prospect remembers. |
| Fresh perspective | A new setting, proof, witness, contrast, or use case for the same promise. |
| Catalog | A condensed list of performances, benefits, or applications near the close. |
| Central Campaign Idea | The compressed dominant image that holds a campaign together. |
| Rule and Example | A campaign pattern that repeats a verbal rule while varying the illustration. |
| Creative Gamble | Risking a nonliteral headline to force the prospect into body copy. |

## How It Relates To

- **Mass desire**: Intensification does not create desire from nothing; it expands an existing mass desire vertically.
- **Awareness stages**: The amount of explanation and proof depends on what the prospect already knows about the product and solution.
- **Sophistication stages**: Fresh perspectives must avoid phraseology and claims the market has already exhausted.
- **Creative planning**: Intensification is one persuasion process inside the larger planning sequence of desire, identification, and belief.
- **Proof-and-focus**: Later techniques add believability, mechanism, concentration, and camouflage when desire alone is not enough.

## Common Misconceptions

- **Myth**: Intensification means saying the same benefit louder.
  **Reality**: It means restating the promise through genuinely new images and perspectives.

- **Myth**: Long copy works because it is long.
  **Reality**: Long copy works only while each added perspective deepens desire without boredom.

- **Myth**: Specific detail is only proof.
  **Reality**: Specific detail also gives desire texture and lets the prospect mentally experience ownership.

- **Myth**: Campaign repetition should keep the same headline.
  **Reality**: Campaign continuity should preserve the central image while varying the execution enough to renew attention.
