# Intensification Patterns

Reusable patterns for applying Schwartz's thirteen ways to strengthen desire.

## Pattern: Direct Fulfillment Build

**Intent:** Turn a vague want into a vivid ownership scene.

**Use when:**
- The product has visible, sensory, or countable results.
- The market wants the outcome but has not fully imagined it.
- You need to make price feel smaller by making the benefit feel larger.

**Skeleton:**

```text
Direct claim -> sensory detail -> product in action -> first ownership moment -> social or personal payoff
```

**Watch:** Do not add secondary benefits until the main image is sharp.

---

## Pattern: Demonstration-Test

**Intent:** Make the prospect imagine proving the claim himself.

**Use when:**
- The result can be checked, compared, measured, or felt quickly.
- Skepticism is present but a simple action can lower it.
- The demonstration itself is dramatic.

**Skeleton:**

```text
Before condition -> reader action -> after condition -> meaning of the result
```

**Watch:** The test must be specific; vague "try it and see" language weakens the move.

---

## Pattern: Time-Expansion Sequence

**Intent:** Show one product producing fresh satisfactions across time.

**Use when:**
- Benefits unfold in stages.
- The product improves, protects, teaches, grows, or compounds results.
- You need to replace a one-time benefit with a future stream of value.

**Skeleton:**

```text
First visible change -> continued automatic action -> later larger payoff -> recognition by reader or others
```

**Watch:** Keep each time stage materially different from the last.

---

## Pattern: Witness Intensification

**Intent:** Refresh the central benefit through other people's reactions.

**Use when:**
- The result has social, status, performance, or expert-evaluation value.
- The prospect wants recognition or reassurance.
- The claim becomes more vivid when someone else is surprised.

**Skeleton:**

```text
Result scene -> witnesses enter -> astonishment or approval -> meaning for the prospect
```

**Watch:** Expert approval should show informed astonishment, not generic endorsement.

---

## Pattern: Before-and-After Contrast

**Intent:** Intensify attraction by making the old condition repellent.

**Use when:**
- The prospect tolerates an inferior product, hidden waste, or preventable frustration.
- The product's superiority can be shown item by item.
- A visible enemy makes the new solution clearer.

**Skeleton:**

```text
Old condition -> cost of old condition -> new solution in parallel -> relief and replacement benefit
```

**Watch:** Paint the black side only when the product can credibly solve it.

---

## Pattern: Ease-to-Outcome Contrast

**Intent:** Magnify the payoff by minimizing the required effort.

**Use when:**
- The prospect believes the result should require work, skill, money, or inconvenience.
- The product has simple application, installation, use, or access.
- The ease itself is a strong part of the appeal.

**Skeleton:**

```text
Simple action -> time or effort required -> multiple benefits created -> avoided labor or cost
```

**Watch:** Do not let ease imply trivial value; make the payoff larger than the effort.

---

## Pattern: Metaphor and Personification

**Intent:** Dramatize fact through imagination without abandoning the claim.

**Use when:**
- The mechanism is invisible, instructional, technical, or hard to feel.
- A literal explanation would be flat.
- The metaphor makes the benefit easier to visualize.

**Skeleton:**

```text
Literal function -> analogy or living role -> repeated imagined action -> practical result
```

**Watch:** The metaphor must clarify the mechanism, not replace it with decoration.

---

## Pattern: Closing Magnitude Stack

**Intent:** Use the closing section to multiply the perceived size of the offer.

**Use when:**
- The main desire has already been developed.
- You need a final push before action.
- The product has many applications, performances, or guaranteed outcomes.

**Skeleton:**

```text
Catalog of uses -> breadth or number -> guarantee repeats core performances -> action request
```

**Watch:** Catalogs work best late; early cataloging can dilute the dominant desire.

---

## Pattern: Campaign Intensification

**Intent:** Carry the same dominant image through repeated advertising without making the campaign unreadable.

**Use when:**
- The product requires frequent national or brand advertising.
- A single advertisement cannot or should not carry the full story.
- The campaign must preserve memory while renewing attention.

**Skeleton:**

```text
Central Campaign Idea -> continuity cue -> fresh disguise -> one new facet of desire
```

**Variations:**
- **Openly repeated idea:** Repeat the central verbal image while varying the opening demonstration.
- **Rule and Example:** Repeat the verbal rule while changing the main illustration.
- **Fixed focal point:** Keep the product, appeal, or style constant while each ad reveals another fact.
- **Creative Gamble:** Use a headline that withholds the product or mechanism to force body-copy reading.

**Watch:** The central idea may stop being the headline as the campaign develops; novelty often has to move the reader into the sales message.

## Pattern Selection Guide

| Situation | Recommended Pattern |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Prospect wants the result but cannot picture it | Direct Fulfillment Build |
| Skepticism can be lowered by simple proof | Demonstration-Test |
| Benefits compound or unfold over time | Time-Expansion Sequence |
| Social reaction or authority matters | Witness Intensification |
| Old solution is visibly inferior | Before-and-After Contrast |
| Ease is a major part of the promise | Ease-to-Outcome Contrast |
| Literal explanation feels flat | Metaphor and Personification |
| Offer needs a final breadth push | Closing Magnitude Stack |
| Repeated campaign must stay fresh | Campaign Intensification |
