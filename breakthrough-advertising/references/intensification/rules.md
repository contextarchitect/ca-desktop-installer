# Intensification Rules

Use these rules to strengthen desire in body copy, campaign executions, and closing sections without sliding into empty repetition.

## Core Rules

### 1. Start From One Dominant Desire

Choose the desire the ad will magnify before adding scenes, examples, or proof.

- Every added passage should point back to the same central fulfillment.
- Do not collect benefits randomly; arrange them around the prospect's strongest want.
- If a passage builds a different desire, move it to another ad or cut it.

### 2. Give Vague Desire a Concrete Goal

Translate the prospect's blurred want into visible, sensory, specific scenes.

- Show what the product looks like, produces, removes, makes easier, or makes possible.
- Use numbers, measurements, sequence, duration, and physical detail.
- Let the prospect see the future the product creates.

### 3. Reinforce, Do Not Repeat

Never restate the same fulfillment in the same way.

- After a direct description, show action.
- After action, bring in the reader.
- After reader involvement, add a test, audience, expert, contrast, or guarantee.
- If the next angle does not feel new, remove it.

### 4. Walk Around the Product

Present the product from every legitimate place where it enters the prospect's life.

- Appearance and tangible form.
- Use and performance.
- First day of ownership.
- Long-term result.
- Social reaction.
- Expert reaction.
- Old problem versus new solution.
- Effort required to get the benefit.

### 5. Make Specificity Do Emotional Work

Use detail not only to prove, but to intensify.

- A number should enlarge the picture, not merely decorate it.
- A sensory detail should let the prospect feel ownership.
- A sequence should make the product seem active and inevitable.

### 6. Use Negative Motivation Deliberately

When appropriate, paint the old problem in full color before presenting the product.

- Irritate the wound only when the product can credibly heal it.
- Keep the negative picture parallel to the positive solution.
- Use repulsion from the old condition and attraction toward the new condition together.

### 7. Turn Proof Moments Into Participation

Where possible, let the reader imagine testing the claim himself.

- Give exact steps: what to inspect, do, compare, or measure.
- Show the immediate result the reader should notice.
- Let the test become a miniature ownership experience.

### 8. Balance Timing and Boredom

Continue adding perspectives only while they renew interest.

- More scenes help when each one changes the emotional angle.
- More scenes hurt when the reader feels the same paragraph returning.
- Re-read after a delay to find the point where reinforcement becomes repetition.

### 9. Adapt Intensification to the Medium

Use the same desire-building logic differently by space and format.

- Small-space, radio, television, and catalog copy require compression.
- Direct mail and large-space ads can carry the full sequence.
- Campaigns can distribute intensification across multiple executions.

### 10. Preserve Campaign Continuity Through Variation

For repeated advertising, hold the central image steady while changing its presentation.

- Do not run the same headline or lead picture until it becomes invisible.
- Vary the analogy, illustration, proof fact, contradiction, or use case.
- Let past desire accumulate while each new ad feels fresh enough to read.

## The Thirteen Intensification Moves

| Move | Rule |
|------|------|
| First presentation | State the claim or satisfaction directly and completely. |
| Product in action | Show the mechanism or use producing the result. |
| Reader inside scene | Describe what happens to the prospect when he owns it. |
| Reader test | Give the prospect a way to prove the claim. |
| Time extension | Show the benefit unfolding over longer time. |
| Audience reaction | Add witnesses whose responses refresh the image. |
| Expert approval | Let informed surprise add force and believability. |
| Contrast | Compare old and new item by item. |
| Black side | Vividly show the problem the product removes. |
| Ease | Magnify the payoff by minimizing the effort. |
| Metaphor | Personify or analogize the fact to dramatize it. |
| Catalog | Stack applications or desires in condensed form. |
| Guarantee | Make the risk reversal summarize the promised performances. |

## Guidelines

- Use Schwartz's dated original terms when referencing the system: prospect, mail order copy, housewife, reader.
- Keep the central claim recognizable even when the surrounding scene changes.
- Let examples carry emotional weight; do not explain every image immediately after presenting it.
- Use audience and expert reactions when the product's result benefits from social validation.
- Use catalog summary late, after the main desire has already been developed in depth.
- Use guarantee intensification at the close, when the prospect is deciding whether to act.

## Exceptions

- **Very short formats**: Use one compressed image, not the full thirteen-move sequence.
- **Highly sophisticated markets**: Avoid familiar superlatives and intensify through fresher proof, mechanism, or contrast.
- **Low-believability claims**: Pair intensification with proof, mechanism, or gradualization before expanding the claim too far.
- **Campaign work**: Spread the moves across executions instead of forcing them into one advertisement.

## Quick Reference

| Rule | Summary |
|------|---------|
| One dominant desire | Intensification needs a single emotional target. |
| Concrete scenes | Desire grows when fulfillment becomes visible. |
| Fresh perspectives | Repeat the promise only by changing the angle. |
| Specificity | Numbers, sequence, and sensory detail deepen want. |
| Participation | Reader tests and ownership scenes make desire personal. |
| Contrast | Old pain and new relief sharpen each other. |
| Compression | Short media need distilled images or campaign repetition. |
| Campaign variation | Preserve the central image; vary the execution. |
