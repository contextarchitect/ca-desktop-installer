# Mass Desire Checklist

Use this checklist before writing copy to verify that a real, usable Mass Desire is present and can be focused onto the product.

## Before You Start

- [ ] You can state the private want in one plain sentence.
- [ ] The desire exists before the ad; it is not just something the advertiser wishes people wanted.
- [ ] There are enough reachable people who share the desire to repay the selling cost.
- [ ] The product has at least one performance that can credibly satisfy the desire.

## Desire Reality

- [ ] The desire shows up in current behavior, purchases, complaints, conversations, search, media, or category movement.
- [ ] The desire is created by forces larger than this single advertiser: social, economic, technological, cultural, instinctive, or trend forces.
- [ ] The market is not being asked to reverse an active preference.
- [ ] The desire can be acknowledged without long explanation.

## Three-Dimension Score

Rate each dimension before choosing the lead appeal.

| Dimension | Strong | Weak |
|-----------|--------|------|
| Urgency | Prospect wants relief, gain, status, or satisfaction soon. | Prospect can postpone without pain or loss. |
| Staying power | Desire repeats or cannot be fully satiated. | Desire is one-time, rare, or easily exhausted. |
| Scope | Enough people share it to support profitable selling. | Desire is too narrow for the offer economics. |

## Market Force Type

- [ ] If the desire is a permanent Mass Instinct, the copy has a fresh angle or fresh proof.
- [ ] If the desire is a mass technological problem, the copy shows why this product solves or improves the unresolved problem.
- [ ] If the desire is a trend, the timing is clear: beginning, fulfillment, or reversal.
- [ ] If the desire comes through Mass Education, there is evidence the shift has become statistically significant.

## Product Fit

- [ ] All major product performances have been listed.
- [ ] Each performance is mapped to the desire it satisfies.
- [ ] One dominant performance has been selected for the ad.
- [ ] Secondary performances support the dominant performance instead of competing with it.
- [ ] Physical facts are translated into proof of performance, not used as the main appeal.

## Headline Readiness

- [ ] The headline can express one dominant thought.
- [ ] The headline starts at the prospect's present awareness: product, desire, or problem.
- [ ] The headline acknowledges, reinforces, or offers satisfaction of the desire.
- [ ] The headline does not require the prospect to understand the product's construction before wanting the result.

## Red Flags

Stop and address if you find:

- The copy must convince people to want the category before they can want the product.
- The selected appeal contradicts a visible market trend.
- The product's physical facts are being treated as the reason to buy.
- Multiple desires are fighting for headline control.
- The market desire is real, but the product cannot credibly satisfy it.
- The desire has scope but little urgency, staying power, or economic value.

## Quick Reference

| Aspect | Ideal | Acceptable | Red Flag |
|--------|-------|------------|----------|
| Existing desire | Already visible in the market | Supported by credible market signals | Invented by the advertiser |
| Urgency | Immediate pressure to satisfy | Clear but delayed motivation | Mild curiosity only |
| Staying power | Repeats or resists satiation | Periodic need | One-time whim |
| Scope | Large or highly profitable audience | Smaller reachable niche | Too few reachable buyers |
| Product fit | One performance directly satisfies desire | Performance needs explanation | Product cannot satisfy desire |
| Headline focus | One dominant desire and performance | One lead plus support | Several competing appeals |
