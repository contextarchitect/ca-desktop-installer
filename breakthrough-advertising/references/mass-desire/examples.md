# Mass Desire Examples

Examples from Schwartz's Chapter 1, preserved in original product and market context with light cleanup and paraphrase.

### Example 1: Chrysler - running against the longer, lower, wider car desire

> In 1948, the public wanted a longer, lower, wider car as a visible sign of a rising standard of living. Chrysler offered a fine functional car with more inside room, but with a shorter, squatter outside profile. Even a large campaign from a creative agency could not overcome the opposing mass desire.

**Principle illustrated:** Copy cannot fight the market's dominant desire. The appeal must move with the desire the public already wants to satisfy.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, ad-concepts

### Example 2: Ford safety campaign - choosing safety while the market wanted horsepower

> By 1954, drivers were judging cars by horsepower. The Twin-H Hudson Hornet, twin-exhaust Cadillac, and Chrysler 300 exploited the horsepower demand. Ford spent heavily to sell safety and met disinterest, then returned the next year with the highest-horsepower engines in its history.

**Principle illustrated:** The strongest built-in product appeal changes with the market. Choosing the wrong desire makes even a reasonable benefit weak.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, headlines-universal

### Example 3: Edsel - fighting the shift toward compact cars

> Schwartz describes the Edsel as a good car with a flood of fine advertising, but it died while the market was switching toward cheap, simple, inexpensive-to-run compact cars.

**Principle illustrated:** Good product and good advertising do not save an appeal that runs against the direction of mass demand.

**Relevance tag:** ad-concepts, funnel-copy

### Example 4: Fine antiques and weight loss - different scopes of desire

> The desire to own fine antiques may create a market of only a few thousand people. The desire to lose weight may be shared by tens of millions. Both can be markets, but their selling economics differ radically.

**Principle illustrated:** Scope is one of the three dimensions of desire. A market is not just a desire; it is a desire shared by enough people to repay the cost of reaching and selling them.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, funnel-copy

### Example 5: Attractiveness, virility, and health - permanent Mass Instinct

> Schwartz names the desire of women to be attractive, men to be virile, and both men and women to keep their health as permanent instincts. The desire does not fade, so the copy writer must create a fresh appeal and stronger believability.

**Principle illustrated:** Permanent desires are powerful but crowded. The copy problem is differentiation, freshness, and belief.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, email-copy, headlines-universal

### Example 6: Television reception, mufflers, and aspirin - mass technological problems

> Bad television reception, corroding automobile mufflers, and slow aspirin relief are technological problems that keep buyers trying products until the problem is solved.

**Principle illustrated:** A recurring unsolved problem creates a durable desire for relief. The same claim may need a new way of being stated or proved.

**Relevance tag:** ad-concepts, video-scripts, funnel-copy

### Example 7: Horsepower, gas economy, and swimming pools - forces of change

> Schwartz points to the horsepower appeal of the Fifties, its later subordination to gas economy, and the decision to display a pay raise with a backyard swimming pool instead of a bigger car as examples of changing trends.

**Principle illustrated:** Trend desire rises, fulfills, and reverses. The copy writer must sense which product appeal should be stressed now.

**Relevance tag:** ad-angles, ad-concepts

### Example 8: Automobile performances - one product, many desires

> A car can offer transportation, dependability, economy, power, recognition, value, novelty, and more. Each performance reaches a different desire and a distinct public, but an ad can effectively lead with only one.

**Principle illustrated:** Product analysis means listing performances, mapping them to desires, and selecting the dominant performance for the headline.

**Relevance tag:** ad-concepts, funnel-copy, headlines-universal

### Example 9: Rolls-Royce and Helena Rubenstein - physical facts as belief support

> Physical facts can sharpen belief after the performance is chosen: a Rolls-Royce suggests riding silence because metal chassis parts are shielded by rubber; Helena Rubenstein's new face cream suggests younger-looking skin because it contains the placenta of living animals. Steel doors, impurity-removal steps, ceramic mufflers, chemically-protected paper, and quick-frozen food all document or prolong a promised performance.

**Principle illustrated:** Physical product facts do not sell by themselves. They justify, document, assure, dramatize, or make believable the functional product.

**Relevance tag:** ad-concepts, video-scripts, funnel-copy
