# Mass Desire Rules

Use these rules to choose the desire, product performance, and headline direction before writing the ad.

## Core Rules

### 1. Do not try to create Mass Desire

Base the ad on desire that already exists in the market.

- Treat desire as market energy, not copy invention.
- If the public has not yet developed the want, the assignment is education, not advertising.
- Do not fight a dominant trend because the product or client prefers another appeal.

### 2. Choose one dominant desire

Every product can appeal to several desires, but the ad can lead with only one.

- Score each desire by urgency, staying power, and scope.
- Select the desire with the greatest economic force at the moment the ad runs.
- Let the headline embody this choice.

### 3. Match the headline to present awareness

Start where the prospect's mind already is.

- If the prospect knows the product and knows it satisfies the desire, start with the product.
- If the prospect knows the desire but not the product, start with the desire.
- If the prospect only feels a general problem, start with that problem and crystallize it into a specific need.

### 4. Sell the functional product

Lead with what the product does for the prospect.

- Do not make plant size, material weight, construction care, or manufacturing pride the primary appeal.
- Translate physical attributes into performances.
- Make the performance the bridge between market desire and product.

### 5. Use physical facts only after the main promise is clear

Physical product facts support the performance claim.

- Use them to justify price.
- Use them to document quality.
- Use them to assure continuing performance over time.
- Use them to sharpen the reader's mental picture of the benefit.
- Use them to create a fresh basis for believability in a crowded field.

### 6. Inventory all product performances before choosing

List what the product can do, then group each performance against the desire it satisfies.

- A car may sell transportation, dependability, economy, power, recognition, value, novelty, and more.
- Do not assume the manufacturer's favorite feature is the strongest selling performance.
- Hidden or unadmitted desires may matter as much as obvious practical benefits.

### 7. Time the appeal to the market's direction

Forces of change require timing.

- Watch for the beginning, fulfillment, and reversal of trends.
- Detect when a shift becomes statistically significant.
- Change the emphasized product appeal when the market's dominant desire changes.

## Guidelines

Less strict recommendations:

- Study mass desires every working day; Schwartz treats detection as a permanent copy writer responsibility.
- Look for desire in social, economic, technological, and cultural forces larger than the advertiser.
- In permanent instinct fields, assume competitors have already made the same promise; look for a fresher mechanism or proof.
- In trend fields, do not wait until every competitor has made the shift obvious.
- Use secondary performances as proof, texture, and support, not as competing headlines.

## Exceptions

When these rules may be relaxed:

- **Deliberate education campaign**: If the goal is long-term public education rather than immediate profitable advertising, the economics change. Do not expect the Amplification Effect.
- **Tiny but profitable market**: Scope can be small if the reachable buyers are valuable enough to repay the selling cost.
- **Late-stage body copy**: Physical facts may become prominent after the ad has already established the desired performance and why it matters.

## Quick Reference

| Rule | Summary |
|------|---------|
| Do not create desire | Find desire already present in the market. |
| Choose one desire | Lead with the strongest desire by urgency, staying power, and scope. |
| Match awareness | Start with product, desire, or problem depending on what the prospect knows. |
| Sell function | Promise what the product does, not what it is made of. |
| Support with facts | Use physical facts to prove and sharpen the performance claim. |
| Inventory performances | Map every product action to a possible desire before selecting one. |
| Time the trend | Shift appeals as the market's dominant desire shifts. |
