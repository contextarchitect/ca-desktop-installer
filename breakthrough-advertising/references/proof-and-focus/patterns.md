# Proof + Focus Patterns

Reusable combinations for Redefinition, Mechanization, Concentration, and Camouflage.

## Pattern: Liability Into Asset

### Intent

Turn a product drawback into evidence that the product works.

### When to Use

- The drawback is visible, noticeable, or already known.
- The drawback cannot be removed without weakening the product.
- The drawback can credibly signal strength, purity, seriousness, or effectiveness.

### Structure

```text
Prospect problem -> ordinary products fail -> drawback becomes proof -> product is the stronger answer
```

### Example

Lifebuoy's medicinal odor becomes proof of odor-destroying power.

### Benefits

- Converts resistance into evidence.
- Prevents the prospect from discovering the objection unframed.

## Pattern: Hard Task Into Simple Adjustment

### Intent

Make a difficult or technical action feel familiar and manageable.

### When to Use

- The product is easy but the category is believed to be hard.
- The prospect fears using the product incorrectly.
- The mechanism cannot be introduced raw without disbelief.

### Structure

```text
Accepted problem -> familiar analogy -> rename symptoms -> isolate rare hard cases -> introduce simple action
```

### Example

TV repairs become warning signals and minor outside adjustments.

### Benefits

- Lowers fear before the product is introduced.
- Allows a later mechanism claim to be accepted.

## Pattern: Promise Then Mechanism

### Intent

Answer the prospect's "How?" exactly when desire begins to create skepticism.

### When to Use

- The promise is attractive but not self-evident.
- The market needs enough reason-why to keep believing.
- The mechanism is not strong enough to lead but strong enough to prove.

### Structure

```text
Promise -> intensified promise -> rising question -> mechanism -> renewed promise
```

### Example

Rinso promises a whiter wash without hard work, then explains how suds float dirt and stains away.

### Benefits

- Keeps mechanism copy selling.
- Prevents desire from turning into disbelief.

## Pattern: Mechanism As Headline

### Intent

Use a new way, process, ingredient, or contrast as the main attraction.

### When to Use

- The market has heard the same promise too many times.
- Competitors make similar claims at similar prices.
- The mechanism is dramatic, novel, or superior enough to carry the ad.

### Structure

```text
New mechanism headline -> why old way fails -> how new way works -> proof -> promise
```

### Example

"Run your car without spark plugs" leads with the mechanism because it offers a new route to power and mileage.

### Benefits

- Restores freshness in a sophisticated market.
- Gives the prospect a new chance to satisfy an old desire.

## Pattern: Bad-Good Concentration

### Intent

Break loyalty to the old solution through repeated one-for-one contrast.

### When to Use

- The competitor has clear feature weaknesses.
- Your product has matching strengths.
- The market is already using the old solution.

### Structure

```text
Old weakness -> new strength -> old weakness -> new strength -> documented superiority
```

### Example

Fire injector copy contrasts spark plug air gaps, carbon, weak sparks, and premium gas with surface firing, no servicing, heavy flame, and regular-gas savings.

### Benefits

- Makes comparison feel concrete and fair.
- Converts each competitor weakness into a reason to switch.

## Pattern: Failure Then Hero Product

### Intent

Make the prospect question a habitual solution before presenting the product.

### When to Use

- The competitor cannot be broken into simple point-by-point contrasts.
- The prospect has lived through a recurring failure sequence.
- The new product must replace a habit or loyalty.

### Structure

```text
Old method defined -> familiar failure dramatized -> cause made logical -> product foreshadowed -> new mechanism and proof
```

### Example

The reducing pill ad defines ordinary plans as passive, dramatizes will-power collapse, then introduces an active medical answer.

### Benefits

- Uses the prospect's own experience as proof.
- Makes switching feel protective rather than impulsive.

## Pattern: Borrowed Format Trust

### Intent

Make an ad enter through the trust already attached to the medium.

### When to Use

- The publication, channel, letter type, or show format is trusted.
- The audience expects a specific editorial or correspondence style.
- The product needs extra believability more than visual distinctiveness.

### Structure

```text
Trusted format cues -> trusted phraseology -> smooth transition -> selling content
```

### Example

The Wall Street Journal version of a handling-people ad uses the Journal's headline, subheads, bars, column treatment, and illustration style.

## Pattern Selection Guide

| Situation | Recommended Pattern |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Product has a visible drawback | Liability Into Asset |
| Product sounds hard to use | Hard Task Into Simple Adjustment |
| Promise is attractive but needs reason-why | Promise Then Mechanism |
| Market is sophisticated and claims are tired | Mechanism As Headline |
| Competitor weaknesses map directly to strengths | Bad-Good Concentration |
| Prospect has repeated failure with old method | Failure Then Hero Product |
| Medium carries strong trust | Borrowed Format Trust |
