# Proof + Focus Rules

Use these rules to remove objections, prove claims, challenge alternatives, and borrow believability.

## Core Rules

### 1. Redefine the Roadblock Before It Kills the Sale

Find the objection built into the product and change the frame around it.

- If the product sounds too hard, redefine the task as simple and familiar.
- If the appeal is too narrow, redefine the product as a gateway to a larger reward.
- If price is too high, redefine the comparison standard.
- If a liability cannot be removed, ask whether it can become proof of strength.

### 2. Simplify by Replacing the Prospect's Mental Picture

Do not merely say "easy." Prove ease by changing what the prospect thinks the act involves.

- Rename difficult actions as familiar actions.
- Separate rare hard cases from frequent simple cases.
- Compare unfamiliar machinery to familiar bodily, household, or daily experiences.
- Avoid the frightening term until the new frame is accepted.

### 3. Escalate by Broadening Consequences

Make the product matter in more of the prospect's life.

- Show more situations where the product pays off.
- Tie a small product to a large investment, ambition, or social outcome.
- Shift from narrow category benefit to broader life benefit.
- Feature the aspect with the widest desired reward.

### 4. Reduce Price Psychologically

Price is judged against a frame. Change the frame.

- Compare against a higher standard when legitimate.
- Break the price against savings, avoided costs, use-frequency, or value received.
- Give a reason the lower price is available.
- Use conditions or favors to make the bargain feel specific, not generic.

### 5. Match Mechanism Depth to Market Need

Ask how much mechanism the prospect needs.

- Name the mechanism if it is already familiar and accepted.
- Describe the mechanism if the prospect wants to know how the promise works.
- Feature the mechanism if the market is sophisticated or competitors sound alike.
- Keep mechanism copy emotional and benefit-loaded.

### 6. Mechanize Bargains and Price Cuts

A discount without a reason is often disbelieved.

- Explain the external event, production change, overstock, timing, or condition behind the price.
- Build the reason before announcing the bargain when believability matters.
- Let quality claims run beside bargain claims so the lower price does not imply lower value.

### 7. Concentrate Only When You Can Provide the Cure

Never attack an alternative unless your product supplies the missing solution.

- State the weakness in the old method.
- Show why that weakness matters to the prospect.
- Immediately contrast your product's compensating strength.
- Use documentation and mechanism to make the contrast fair.

### 8. Borrow Believability From Trusted Channels

When the medium has trust, use its trust-bearing conventions.

- Match headline, subhead, illustration, spacing, and transition patterns where appropriate.
- Adopt news, report, correspondence, or documentary phraseology when the audience believes those forms.
- In direct mail, borrow from the correspondence the prospect opens without hesitation.
- In radio or TV, borrow the trusted sound and structure of news or documentary forms.

### 9. Use Mood to Escape Advertising Skepticism

If the market resists hard-sell language, change the emotional surface.

- Use understatement: fewer adjectives, shorter sentences, quieter claims.
- Use deadly sincerity: name the sacrifice, limitation, or selectiveness before the benefit.
- Let restraint itself become evidence of truthfulness.

## Guidelines

Less strict recommendations:

- Redefinition and Gradualization often work together; delay the new frame until the prospect has accepted its premises.
- Mechanism can be a small phrase, a paragraph, a headline, or the entire structure of the ad.
- Concentration usually needs more space than a pure promise because it must fairly weaken an existing loyalty.
- Camouflage should fit the actual medium; generic imitation weakens trust.

## Exceptions

- **Dominant product story**: If the product's own promise is fresh and overwhelming, avoid giving competitors attention through Concentration.
- **Known commodity**: If the mechanism is familiar, extended explanation can bore the prospect.
- **Low-trust medium**: Camouflage cannot borrow trust from a channel the prospect already distrusts.

|------|---------|
| Redefine roadblocks | Change the meaning of the objection. |
| Prove ease | Replace "hard" with a familiar mental model. |
