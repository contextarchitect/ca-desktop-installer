# Market Sophistication Checklist

Use this checklist before writing or auditing a headline in a competitive market.

## Before You Start

- [ ] Gather a sample of competing ads in the field.
- [ ] Identify the main mass desire the market is already trying to satisfy.
- [ ] Identify the prospect's awareness stage separately; do not confuse awareness with sophistication.
- [ ] Note whether the field is a main market or a newly opened submarket.

## Competitive Evidence

- [ ] Are there no meaningful competing ads for this specific product promise?
- [ ] Are competitors repeating the same direct claim?
- [ ] Are competitors making the claim bigger, faster, easier, or more measurable?
- [ ] Are competitors leading with new processes, discoveries, ingredients, devices, or methods?
- [ ] Are competitors elaborating those mechanisms with extra benefits or stronger versions?
- [ ] Are competitors abandoning claims and relying on image, role, status, taboo, fear, or aspiration?

## Stage Diagnosis

- [ ] **First Stage candidate**: The promise is new to this market and a direct claim still feels fresh.
- [ ] **Second Stage candidate**: The direct claim works, but competitors are already enlarging it.
- [ ] **Third Stage candidate**: Prospects know the promise and need a new reason to believe it.
- [ ] **Fourth Stage candidate**: The market has accepted a mechanism, and competitors now compete on versions of that mechanism.
- [ ] **Fifth Stage candidate**: Prospects are tired of the category and resist both claims and mechanisms.

## Headline Fit

- [ ] If First Stage: Does the headline name the need or claim without unnecessary cleverness?
- [ ] If Second Stage: Does the headline enlarge a proven claim while staying believable?
- [ ] If Third Stage: Does the headline make a new mechanism dominant?
- [ ] If Fourth Stage: Does the headline improve or enlarge the current mechanism?
- [ ] If Fifth Stage: Does the headline or image create identification before selling?

## Believability Checks

- [ ] Is the claim still within what the prospect can accept?
- [ ] Does the mechanism sound legitimate and significant, not decorative?
- [ ] Would the prospect see this as a fresh chance rather than another version of the same disappointment?
- [ ] Is the promise supported after the headline by proof, explanation, or demonstration?

## Red Flags

Stop and re-diagnose if you find:

- The headline makes a first-stage direct claim in a market full of similar claims.
- The copy enlarges a promise that prospects already discount automatically.
- The mechanism is new to the writer but not new to the market.
- The ad explains a mechanism when prospects are already avoiding the category.
- The campaign treats a new submarket as if it has the same sophistication as the parent category.

## Quick Reference

| Signal | Likely Stage | Best Next Move | Red Flag |
|--------|--------------|----------------|----------|
| No similar ads | First | State the claim directly. | Over-clever headline. |
| Claims are escalating | Second | Outbid with care. | Unbelievable promise. |
| Claims are discounted | Third | Introduce mechanism. | More exaggeration. |
| Mechanisms are escalating | Fourth | Improve mechanism. | Decorative novelty. |
| Category is ignored | Fifth | Use identification. | More product argument. |
