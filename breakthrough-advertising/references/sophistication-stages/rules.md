# Sophistication Stages Rules

Use these rules to choose the right copy and headline move after the market's sophistication stage has been diagnosed.

## Core Rules

### 1. Research the Competitive Field First

Before writing, gather current and historical competing ads in the field.

- Look for repeated claims, repeated mechanisms, and repeated proof structures.
- Identify the latest approach that is still producing sales.
- Treat the market's advertising history as evidence, not as background color.

### 2. Match the Headline to the Stage

Use the stage labels defined in `knowledge.md`; do not choose a move because it sounds more creative.

| Stage | Primary Move | Avoid |
|-------|--------------|-------|
| First | Name the need or claim directly. | Cleverness, indirection, over-explanation. |
| Second | Enlarge the successful claim. | Merely repeating the first claim. |
| Third | Lead with a new mechanism. | Piling on more promise without a new reason to believe. |
| Fourth | Enlarge or improve the mechanism. | Treating the old mechanism as still fresh. |
| Fifth | Use identification to bring the prospect into the ad. | More claims, more mechanisms, or louder selling. |

### 3. In a First-Stage Market, Be Simple and Direct

When the market has not heard the promise before, the headline should name the need or the claim.

- State the desire plainly.
- Dramatize the claim in the copy.
- Introduce the product only after the prospect recognizes the desired result.
- Prove that the product works.

### 4. In a Second-Stage Market, Outbid the Existing Claim

When the direct claim is still producing sales, copy the structure of the winning claim and enlarge it.

- Push the promise further: faster, bigger, easier, more certain, or more measurable.
- Use concrete numbers where the result can be measured.
- Stop before the claim breaks believability or invites automatic discounting.

### 5. Watch for Second-Stage Collapse

The enlargement race eventually destroys its own credibility.

- Words become inflated and less meaningful.
- Prospects discount claims before reading the proof.
- Competitors compensate by making still larger promises.
- Regulation, skepticism, and falling response often follow.

### 6. In a Third-Stage Market, Shift from Promise to Mechanism

When prospects have heard all the claims, make the mechanism dominant.

- Put the new way, process, discovery, ingredient, or performance in the headline.
- Compress the familiar claim into fewer words or move it into the lead.
- Explain why this mechanism gives the prospect a fresh chance after past disappointment.
- Make the mechanism believable and significant, not merely novel.

### 7. In a Fourth-Stage Market, Enlarge the Mechanism

When a competitor's mechanism is working, embellish the mechanism rather than the base promise.

- Make the mechanism easier, quicker, surer, or more complete.
- Add benefits that the prior mechanism did not seem to solve.
- Remove limitations or inconveniences attached to the earlier mechanism.
- Expect the same believability ceiling that ends the second stage.

### 8. In a Fifth-Stage Market, Stop Arguing and Create Identification

When the field is exhausted, claims and mechanisms are no longer the doorway.

- Lead with the prospect's self-image, role, fear, aspiration, or social identity.
- Use image, situation, or headline to make the prospect recognize himself.
- Let identification reopen attention before any product argument appears.
- Coordinate with the identification technique rather than forcing a direct claim.

## Guidelines

- A new mechanism must be accepted by the market as both legitimate and important.
- A recurring desire can revive even after old claims stop working.
- Do not assume that competitor copying is ethically attractive just because it can be strategically effective.
- If the market is split, write to the segment with the highest profit potential and clearest current stage.
- If a submarket opens inside an exhausted market, diagnose the submarket separately.

## Quick Reference

| Situation | Schwartz Move |
|-----------|---------------|
| No similar ads exist | Make the direct claim. |
| The direct claim still works but competitors appear | Enlarge the claim. |
| Claims are familiar and discounted | Introduce a new mechanism. |
| Mechanisms are now competing | Enlarge the mechanism. |
| Prospects avoid the whole category | Lead with identification. |
