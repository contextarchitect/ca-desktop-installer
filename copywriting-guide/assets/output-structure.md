# Copywriting Guide Output Structure

This is the structural template for the final output. The skill populates each section from extracted inputs. Sections marked [CUSTOMIZED] are populated from avatar research and brand guidelines. Sections marked [UNIVERSAL] are sections whose BASE TEXT this skill authors rather than extracts. Appendices marked [UNIVERSAL, VERBATIM] are file copies of a reference file and are never rewritten, paraphrased or brand-adapted.

**[UNIVERSAL] does not mean brand-free, and no section is one hundred percent universal.** This file previously said sections 1, 5, 11 and 12 were, and a six-folder inventory on 2026-09-09 measured that false in every existing guide:

| Guide | Line | Brand-specific content inside a section this file called universal |
|---|---|---|
| regrowth | 552 | the hard-water check in the humanization checklist |
| everease | 419 | the guarantee-and-proof check in the ultimate test |
| himfresh | 461 | the Shame Check, which fails a piece against a named voice pillar |
| psychic-samira | 577 | the customer-name test in the ultimate test |
| peptiques | 331 | the Moat Map lead rule in the checklist |

**Every section may carry brand-specific content.** The marks below say where a section's base text comes from on a FIRST BUILD. They do not license a by-number regeneration of a guide that already exists: no section is ever regenerated by number on the strength of a skill version change, and no section's text is ever replaced without the replacement being compared against what was there. The exact invariant and the preservation duty that carries it are stated once, in Change policy, and that statement governs.

The universal rule sets do not live inline any more. `../references/humanization-rules.md` and `../references/line-level-rules.md` are embedded verbatim as Appendix C and Appendix D, so a universal change is propagated by replacing the appendix body and the `appendices:` line of the provenance stamp, with no regeneration of any brand section.

**Section titles interpolate the brand.** The titles below carry `[BRAND_NAME]` and `[MECHANISM]` placeholders, and a generated guide resolves them: Section 2 is "The Peptiques Voice" and "THE REGROWTH+ VOICE", Section 10 is "Bladder-Function Messaging Framework" and "Pineal/Fluoride Mechanism Messaging Framework". That is the convention, not a drift. Match a section by what it is about, never by its title string.

## Provenance stamp

The guide's first lines, above the title:

```
generated-by: copywriting-guide v1.5.2
appendices: humanization-rules v1.5.1 sha256:<first 16 hex>, line-level-rules v1.5.1 sha256:<first 16 hex>
generated: [YYYY-MM-DD]
```

This block is the same schema SKILL.md Step 10 states, and the two must stay identical; where they ever differ, Step 10 wins and this file is the bug. Each appendix version is read from the `<!-- universal appendix: ... -->` comment on line 1 of the corresponding reference file, and each digest is the sha256 of that file computed at generation time. **A stamp missing either digest, or carrying a digest that disagrees with the appendix actually embedded, fails the Step 10 verification gate and the guide is not delivered.**

## Appendix E: Brand Extensions

Ruling R9 of 2026-09-09. A brand accumulates sections the template does not produce. Regrowth's guide is the measured case: a HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE preamble, five sections tagged *(new in v2.0)*, and a CHANGELOG, seven in all, added by hand after generation and present in no brand input. A rebuild from inputs cannot reproduce them, because they were never in the inputs.

Appendix E is where they go. It is not the template growing to fit one brand.

**What goes in.** Any section a brand accumulated that the current template does not produce. Carried VERBATIM across a rebuild, never regenerated, never paraphrased.

**What each entry carries.** The section body as it stood, the date it was added, and where it came from (the session, the operator instruction, or "unknown, present in the superseded guide at rebuild time" when the origin cannot be established). An entry with no origin is still carried; an entry with no date is dated to the rebuild that found it.

**The promotion rule.** When TWO brands independently grow the same extension, it becomes a candidate for the template. The operator decides on that evidence. A single brand's extension is never promoted, however good it is: one brand wanting a section is a brand preference, and two brands arriving at the same section without coordination is the only evidence this process has that the template is missing something. Promotion is an operator decision, never an automatic one, and never made from a single brand.

**What counts as "the same extension."** Not the same title. Titles interpolate the brand name and are written by whoever added the section, so matching on them both misses real pairs and invents false ones. Two extensions are the same when BOTH hold:

1. **Same purpose.** They answer the same question for a writer. "THE 5 OBJECTIONS FRAMEWORK" and "Handling Pushback" are the same purpose; "CTA COPYWRITING RULES" and "SOCIAL PROOF GUIDELINES" are not, however adjacent they look.
2. **Same scope.** They cover comparable ground. A section that lists five named objections with approved responses is not the same scope as a paragraph telling the writer to expect objections, and promoting on that pair would put a paragraph in the template while two brands were asking for a framework.

**What counts as "independently."** Each brand's entry carries its date and its origin (Appendix E requires both). Independent means neither was copied from the other and neither came from the same operator instruction applied to both. If the origins show one was cloned from the other, that is ONE brand's extension appearing twice, and it does not promote.

**Fail closed on either judgment.** If you cannot establish that the purpose and scope match, or you cannot establish independent origin, there is NO promotion candidate. Say so and leave both extensions in their own Appendix E. An unresolved judgment is not a weak yes: promoting a section into the template puts it in every future guide for every brand, and the cost of waiting for a third brand is one more guide carrying a section it already has.

## Change policy

Three events change a guide. They do not imply one another, and a release may fire more than one.

**The invariant, stated exactly.** No section of an existing guide is ever regenerated BY NUMBER on the strength of a skill version change. That is the thing the 2026-09-09 measurement killed, and it is trigger 2's whole subject. It is NOT a ban on regenerating a section when the brand inputs behind it changed, which is what trigger 1 is for and has always been for. What every trigger shares is the weaker and more useful duty below: nothing is DISCARDED without being surfaced first.

**The preservation duty, common to all three triggers.** Whenever a trigger replaces the text of a section that already exists, compare the replacement against what was there and surface to the operator anything present in the old and absent from the new. The operator disposes, item by item. This is one paragraph of work on trigger 1 and a whole procedure on trigger 2, because trigger 1 replaces a few sections from known inputs while trigger 2 replaces the entire guide. The duty is the same in both.

**1. Brand inputs change** (avatar research, brand guidelines, business validation, moat dispositions, positioning, and the Phase 4.5 angle roadmap).
Affected sections: 2, 3, 4, 6A, 7, 8, 9, 10, Appendix A and Appendix B. **Appendices C and D are untouched. Appendix E is never regenerated, but it MAY be extended by this trigger**, because content dropped from a regenerated section can be content the template does not produce.

**One sequence, covering both replacement and deletion.** Trigger 1 touches brand sections, which is exactly why they are the sections most likely to hold something a person added by hand that no input records. Run it in this order and do not shortcut it:

1. **Generate a candidate** for each affected section from the changed inputs. Do not write it over the existing section yet.
2. **Compare** each candidate against the section as it stands, and list what is present in the existing section and absent from the candidate.
3. **Get an item-by-item disposition from the operator** on that list. Content the current template does not produce becomes a new Appendix E entry, dated and attributed the same way trigger 2 does it. Content the template does produce, in a form the brand had improved, is folded into the candidate or dropped, by decision.
4. **Then replace**, once the list is disposed of and not before.

**Deletion runs through the same sequence.** Removing `schwartz-applied.md` deletes Section 6A, and Section 6A is entirely brand-specific worked examples. A deletion is a replacement with nothing, so it goes through steps 2, 3 and 4 exactly as a rewrite does: list what 6A held, get a disposition, and only then delete. A section that vanishes because a gate file was removed is brand work lost to a file-existence check, which is the cheapest possible way to lose it.

**This does not breach the Step 7 silence rule, because the two have different audiences.** SKILL.md Step 7 says that when `schwartz-applied.md` is absent, the GUIDE never mentions Schwartz, the seven techniques or the fact that a section was skipped. That governs the delivered document and its reader. The disposition list here is operator-facing working material, not guide content, and it exists precisely so a person decides what happens to the worked examples. Surface the deletion to the OPERATOR; say nothing about it in the GUIDE. A deletion silently applied to both is the failure; a deletion announced in the guide is a different failure.

A trigger-1 pass that silently overwrites Section 3's accumulated brand vocabulary is the same failure as a trigger-2 pass that overwrites Section 5, only smaller.

**Presence or absence of `schwartz-applied.md` is itself an input trigger.** When it appears, generate 6A; when it is removed, delete 6A. Neither event renumbers anything, which is why 6A is lettered.

**2. The copywriting-guide skill's version changes: REBUILD AND COMPARE (ruling R10, 2026-09-09).**

There is no by-number regeneration and no by-title migration. Both were retired on 2026-09-09, on measurement rather than preference:

- By-number regeneration of sections 1, 5, 6, 11 and 12 was retired because every one of those sections carries brand-specific content in every existing guide (the five rows in the legend above). Regenerating them by number destroys brand work everywhere, not just in one outlier.
- The by-title migration was retired because there is no single older layout to migrate from. The existing guides carry four distinct section orders across four generator dates, and one of them was extended by hand to a Version 2.0 with five extra sections and no appendices. A remap by title has nothing stable to remap against.

A skill version change is propagated by REBUILDING the guide, in this order:

1. **Rebuild** the guide from the brand's Phase 1 (business validation), Phase 2 (avatar research), Phase 3 (brand guidelines) and Phase 4.5 (angle roadmap) inputs, using the CURRENT skill, into a new file. The rebuild reads inputs, not the old guide.
2. **Compare** the rebuilt guide against the superseded one, and surface to the operator everything present in the OLD and absent from the NEW. Compare by content, not by heading: the headings differ between layouts and they interpolate the brand name.
3. **The operator decides, item by item.** Content the template does not produce goes to Appendix E. Content the template does produce, in a form the brand had improved, is a candidate to fold back into the rebuilt section. Content that was stale is dropped, by decision and on the record.
4. **The superseded guide is preserved in git history.** It is not deleted separately, and it is never merged in automatically.

**The comparison is a judgment step, not an automated merge.** Tooling may produce the list of what is present in the old and absent from the new. Nothing but the operator decides what happens to a line on that list. A rebuild that silently drops brand work has failed, and the comparison is the only thing standing between a version bump and that outcome.

**When trigger 2 and trigger 3 fire together, trigger 2 SUBSUMES trigger 3.** A release that changes both the skill and an appendix, as v1.5.1 does, runs the rebuild only. Do not run the appendix copy first and then rebuild: a rebuild reads the current appendix files by construction, so trigger 3's work is already done, and running it first mutates the very guide trigger 2 is about to compare against. **The comparison baseline is the superseded guide as it stood BEFORE any part of this release touched it, INCLUDING work that was never committed.** The last revision in git history is not that baseline, and taking it as the baseline is how uncommitted edits get overwritten without ever appearing on the comparison list. Establish the baseline like this, in order:

1. **Check the guide file's git status first.** If it is clean, the last revision IS the on-disk content and either serves.
2. **If it is dirty, the ON-DISK file is the baseline, not the revision.** Snapshot it before the rebuild writes anything: copy it aside and record the sha256 of the copy. Uncommitted edits are still brand work, and they are the work least likely to exist anywhere else.
3. **Fail closed if you cannot establish which.** A rebuild that cannot say what its baseline was, byte for byte, does not start. Recording a revision id documents a baseline; it does not preserve one that was never in the revision.

Record the baseline in the delivery output as the sha256 of the exact bytes compared against, plus the revision id when the file was clean. A comparison against a baseline nobody can name proves nothing, and a comparison against a baseline that silently excluded on-disk work proves less than nothing, because it looks like a clean pass.

**3. An appendix file changes** (`../references/humanization-rules.md` or `../references/line-level-rules.md`).
Appendices C and D are NEVER regenerated by an LLM, and Appendix E is never touched by an appendix change at all. They are replaced by copying the current reference file between its two markers, and the `appendices:` stamp line (version and sha256) is updated. What else moves depends on WHAT changed:

Classify the change as EDITORIAL or SEMANTIC. The test is one question: **could a guide surface that restates this rule now be wrong?**

- **EDITORIAL.** Rewording that leaves every rule's meaning, scope and thresholds where they were. A clearer sentence, a better example, a typo. Propagate the appendix body and the stamp line. Nothing else moves.
- **SEMANTIC.** Anything that changes what a rule requires, permits or is called. This includes but is NOT limited to a threshold, a ratio, a ceiling, rule numbering, tier assignment, the Rule 1 vocabulary table, the Redefinition register format, adding or removing a banned form, and changing a carve-out's conditions. Propagate the appendix body and the stamp line, PLUS every surface in the dependency manifest below.

When you cannot tell which it is, it is semantic. The cost of refreshing five surfaces unnecessarily is a few minutes; the cost of missing one is a guide that contradicts its own appendix.

### Appendix dependency manifest

These surfaces RESTATE appendix semantics rather than referring to them, so a semantic change leaves them contradicting the appendix that was just copied in. Refresh these, and only these, in the same pass:

| Surface | What it restates |
|---------|------------------|
| Section 3 | brand-voiced examples of Rules 2, 5, 12 and 13; brand vocabulary rows that mirror a Rule 1 category |
| Section 5 | the seven literal checks, their thresholds and their pass conditions |
| Section 6 | the connector test and any structural rule that restates a line-level or humanization rule |
| Section 11 | golden-rules summary and the priority order for conflicting guidance |
| Section 12 | the five tests and the scoring rubric, wherever they name a rule or a count |
| Appendix A | the per-pair annotations naming which rules each before/after example applies |
| Appendix B | the non-negotiables table |

Nothing else. The brand's voice pillars, archetypes, content-type guides, messaging framework and before/after examples do not move when an appendix does. That is what keeps trigger 3 cheap, and keeping it cheap is the whole reason the appendices are separate files.
