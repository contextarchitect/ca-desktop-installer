# Universal Line-Level Rules

These rules govern the sentence, the paragraph, the handoff between sections, and the order of the argument. They apply to every brand, every category and every format. Think of them as the Copy stage written as checks: by the time you are here, the research is gathered, the mechanism is settled and the brief is built, so the only decision left is the wording.

This file is a self-contained universal appendix. It is embedded verbatim in generated brand guides and vendored into prompts that do not carry the rest of the skill library, so it never links out to another document.

---

## Sentence rules

- **Vary length hard.** A long sentence followed by a three-word sentence makes rhythm. Uniform length reads as a machine, and it is the first thing a reader feels before they can say why.
- **No sentence should need a second read.** If you have to go back to the start of it to get it, split it in two.
- **Front-load the point.** The most important word goes early, not buried behind a qualifier or a wind-up clause.
- **One idea per sentence.** An "and" joining two ideas is usually a period waiting to happen.

---

## Paragraph rules

- **One to three sentences.** Single-sentence paragraphs are allowed and powerful.
- **One thought per paragraph.** When the thought changes, the paragraph breaks. Two thoughts in one block means the reader picks which one to keep.
- **No wall of text, ever.** White space is what keeps a thumb moving down the page.
- **End on a small open loop where you can.** Every paragraph should pull the eye into the next one rather than closing the subject.

---

## Transition rules

- **Every section hands off with a tension the next section resolves.** The handoff is a sentence, not a heading. The reader should arrive at the next section already wanting the thing it contains.
- **No dead ends.** If the reader could comfortably stop at the end of a section, you have lost them there, and no later section gets to argue.
- **A connector carries a fact or a stake. It never announces one.** "But it gets worse" and "And that's when" hand something forward. "Here's the thing" and "Let's face it" announce that something is coming and then deliver the same sentence you would have written without them.

**The connector test.** Delete the connector and read the sentence again. If the sentence lost information, it was a connector. If it only lost throat-clearing, it was a stall, and the copy is better without it.

---

## The argument order

Five lines, in plain English. They hold for every format, every category and every awareness level:

1. The reader feels the pain before you explain anything.
2. The hidden cause comes before the product.
3. Proof sits beside the claim it proves, not ten paragraphs later.
4. Belief is earned before you ask.
5. One idea per section, one ask per CTA.

### Entry by format

The five lines above never reorder. What changes is where a given format enters them and how fast it moves.

- **An email takes one slice.** One idea, one angle, one ask. An email that tries to run the whole argument runs none of it.
- **A social post makes one move.** Land the pain, or turn on the hidden cause, or show the proof. One of those, not all three.
- **An ad is hook, one mechanism beat, CTA.** Everything else lives on the page the ad points at.

**One authority, two grains.** The five lines above are INVARIANTS: properties any correct argument has, at any length, in any format. They are not a section order and they do not compete with one. The macro-order itself, the named attention-to-offer spine and the entry point each format takes into it, is owned by the funnel builder skill, which is the single authority. Where this file and that spine ever appear to disagree, the spine wins and this file is the one to correct.

What that means in practice: the five lines are enough to write and check an email, a post, an ad, or any individual passage, because each of them is a property you can test on the text in front of you. They are NOT sufficient to lay out a long-form page, because a page also needs the named beats in their named order, and that list is not here and is not summarized here.
