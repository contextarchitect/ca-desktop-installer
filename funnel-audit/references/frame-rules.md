# Editorial Frame Rules (15-Point Pass/Fail Checklist)

Transcribed from the RMBC operational manual, Stage 6 (`_frameworks/rmbc-operational-manual.md`). An advertorial or listicle has to hold its editorial frame. It has to feel like content, not a naked ad, and it has to sequence like an honest article. A "frame break" is any moment the illusion cracks or the argument tips its hand too early.

Run this 15-point pass on any advertorial or listicle. Each rule is phrased as an enforceable PASS/FAIL. **Any FAIL is a rewrite.** Feed each FAIL into the triage rubric (`triage-rubric.md`) so it gets scored and ranked alongside every other finding rather than reported in isolation.

---

## Verdict & reveal timing

1. **Verdict-hold rule.** The final recommendation or winner must NOT appear above the fold or before the problem is established. FAIL if the conclusion is spoiled in the headline or first screen.

2. **Mechanism-before-product rule.** The hidden cause (the Root Cause Narrative) must be revealed before the product is named as the hero. FAIL if the product is pitched before the reader understands why they need it.

3. **Slow-reveal rule.** The pitch escalates; the hard sell does not appear in the first third of the piece. FAIL if a "Buy Now / price / discount" hits before the story earns it.

## Balance & coverage (for listicles/comparisons)

4. **Competitor coverage ratio.** Coverage of your product vs any single competitor must not exceed roughly **2:1 by word count**. FAIL if the "roundup" is 90% your product. It reads as an ad, not a review.

5. **Even-handed structure rule.** Every item in a comparison uses the same fields and criteria in the same order. FAIL if your product gets extra sections competitors do not.

6. **Acknowledge-a-flaw rule.** An honest review names at least one limitation or "who it is not for." FAIL if the piece is 100% positive. Unbroken praise breaks the editorial frame.

## Voice & source integrity

7. **Editorial-voice rule.** Written in third-person / journalistic or first-person-reviewer voice, not brand "we/our" sales voice. FAIL if it slips into first-person-brand pitch mid-article.

8. **Attribution rule.** Claims and stats are attributed to a named source, study, or reviewer. FAIL if big numbers float unsourced.

9. **Testimonial-realism rule.** Quotes read like real people (specific, imperfect, attributed by name/place). FAIL if they are generic superlatives with no specifics.

## Congruence & continuity

10. **Angle-congruence rule.** Headline, hook, mechanism, and offer all point at the SAME single idea. FAIL if the banner sells one thing and the body sells another (the Regrowth+ "summer heat vs. hard water" break).

11. **Single-mechanism rule.** One problem mechanism and one solution mechanism carry the piece. FAIL if two competing explanations appear.

12. **Claim-proof adjacency rule.** Each claim has its proof beside it. FAIL if proof is stranded paragraphs away from the claim it supports.

13. **One-ask rule.** Each CTA makes exactly one request. FAIL on dual CTAs ("Shop Now / See the Science") or paragraph-long asks.

## Transition integrity

14. **No-dead-end rule.** Every section hands off to the next with an open loop or question. FAIL if the reader hits a natural stopping point before the offer.

15. **Escalation rule.** Emotional intensity and specificity rise toward the offer, never plateau or drop. FAIL if the strongest material (e.g. the transformation timeline) is buried after weaker material.

---

## The two canonical Regrowth+ frame breaks

The two frame breaks on the Regrowth+ page are exactly what this checklist is built to catch:

- Verdict/angle spoiled and contradicted by the "summer heat" banner (**rule 10**).
- Lopsided emphasis with a dual CTA (**rules 4 and 13**).
