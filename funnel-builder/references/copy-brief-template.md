# Copy Brief Template (17 Fields)

Transcribed from the RMBC operational manual, Stage 3.1 (`_frameworks/rmbc-operational-manual.md`). The Copy Brief is the bridge between thinking and writing. Once it is built, writing becomes filling in each bullet in order. If Research and Mechanism are done well, the brief is where you assemble the argument; the actual copy is just wording it.

**Consolidate this brief BEFORE writing any copy.** Populate every field from the full funnel-builder input set (see ../SKILL.md "Required Inputs"): the 6 upstream documents (#1-6), the always-required foundational documents (#7 Brand Guidelines, #8 Copywriting Guide, #9 Angle Roadmap), and the Stage 0 brand configuration. Several fields draw from outside the 6 upstream documents: field 15 (Tone & Voice) from #8, field 13 (Offer) from product specifics and brand config, and field 16 (Mandatories & Compliance) from brand config. A field you cannot fill is not a field to invent: it is an **input gap**, and it triggers the skill's Degraded Input Handling (see ../SKILL.md "Degraded Input Handling", which now covers missing foundational docs and brand config as well as inputs #1-6). Flag it to the operator; do not paper over it. Compliance (field 16) is never guessed: see the blocking rule in that section.

## Terminology

This skill uses the ContextArchitect names Root Cause Narrative and Solution Mechanism Narrative; the RMBC framework calls these the Unique Mechanism of the Problem (UMP) and Unique Mechanism of the Solution (UMS), per the canonical precedence in the angle-card schema's "Canonical sources and terminology" section.

## The 17 fields, in order

Each field lists what goes in it and a worked example. The examples are drawn from the Regrowth+ (GCC hair) case and are **labeled examples only**. Replace them with the brand's actual content.

1. **Product Name & Format** - what you are selling and the copy format (VSL, advertorial, long-form sales page, email). Format comes from the Step 0.2 final format decision.
   - *Example:* Regrowth+ Shampoo & Conditioner Bundle. Format: long-form advertorial to sales page.

2. **The Avatar (one sentence)** - the single person you are writing to. From avatar research (Phase 2).
   - *Example:* A 30-something professional who moved to Dubai for a career jump and is quietly panicking about the hair filling their shower drain.

3. **The Core Desire** - the one outcome they want most, in their words. From avatar research desire framework.
   - *Example:* "I want my hair, and the way I show up, back to how it was before I moved here."

4. **The One Throbbing Pain** - the single biggest emotional pain you are leading with. One, not three.
   - *Example:* Watching clumps come out in the shower and fearing they are falling apart at the worst possible time.

5. **Specific Opening Moment** - the concrete scene the lead will drop the reader into.
   - *Example:* The stomach-drop of clearing a fistful of hair from the drain before a big client morning.

6. **Unique Mechanism of the Problem (UMP)** (ContextArchitect name: Root Cause Narrative - primary) - the hidden villain, named. This is the Root Cause Narrative from angle roadmap Step 1A; the angle card's Root Cause Frame selects the per-angle emphasis.
   - *Example:* The mineral cast from desalinated water suffocating follicles, not stress, not genetics.

7. **Unique Mechanism of the Solution (UMS)** (ContextArchitect name: Solution Mechanism Narrative - primary) - how the product uniquely defeats that villain. This is the Solution Mechanism Narrative from angle roadmap Step 1B; the angle card's Mechanism Frame selects the per-angle emphasis.
   - *Example:* Chelating "molecular magnet" lifts the cast; growth actives then reach the follicle.

8. **Big Idea / Angle** - the one-sentence hook that fuses pain + mechanism + promise. From the angle card.
   - *Example:* "It's not you. It's the water, and there's finally a shampoo built to beat it."

9. **Primary Promise** - the concrete result and rough timeframe.
   - *Example:* Less shedding in a week, visibly thicker hair by week six.

10. **Proof Stack** - the specific evidence, ranked strongest first: studies, credentials, before/afters, testimonials, guarantee.
    - *Example:* Chelation science + rosemary-vs-minoxidil study + bundle-user testimonials by city + 90-day money-back.

11. **Objections & Answers** - every "yeah but," each with its rebuttal and where it gets handled. From the Objection Inventory (input #5) and the Step 0.7 objection map.
    - *Example:* "Works on my hair type?" to chelation is universal because the water exposure is universal.

12. **Emotional Payoff / Future Pace** - the after-state you will paint them into.
    - *Example:* Walking into the meeting not thinking about your hair at all; the barber who asks what changed.

13. **The Offer** - exact price, structure, discount, bonuses, guarantee, urgency. From brand config / product specifics.
    - *Example:* Bundle $60 (from $83, save 27%), free UAE shipping, 90-day money-back.

14. **Single Primary CTA** - the one action, worded. One ask, one line.
    - *Example:* "Start protecting your hair, order the bundle."

15. **Tone & Voice Notes** - how it should sound, plus banned words. From the copywriting guide (Phase 4).
    - *Example:* Direct, been-there, slightly clinical-but-human. No hype adjectives.

16. **Mandatories & Compliance** - claims you must / cannot make, legal lines, region rules. From brand config Question Set 2. If brand config is missing, this field is a **BLOCKING placeholder, never a guess**: state that compliance rules are unknown and the page must not ship until the operator supplies them (see ../SKILL.md "Degraded Input Handling"). Compliance mandatories are never generated; a fabricated compliance line is worse than a missing one.
    - *Example:* No permanent-cure claims; "results vary"; keep to structure/function language.

17. **Section-by-Section Outline** - the ordered argument: every beat of the letter, top to bottom, as a bullet list. **This is where the format library's section architecture gets instantiated.** Pull the section sequence for the finalized format (Step 0.2) from `advertorial-framework.md`, `listicle-framework.md`, or the `format-library.md` entry, and write one bullet per beat in the persuasive-spine order (see ../SKILL.md Core Principles, "The Persuasive Spine and Entry Point"). Apply the Step 0.7 objection map so each objection lands in its assigned beat.

## How the brief connects to the rest of the workflow

- Fields 1-16 come from the full input set (the 6 upstream documents #1-6, the foundational documents #7-9, and the Stage 0 brand configuration and planning outputs). If a field cannot be filled, it is an input gap; run Degraded Input Handling.
- Field 17 is the instantiation point: the format decision (Step 0.2) + the format reference file's section architecture + the objection map (Step 0.7) become the ordered outline you then write to.
- The persuasive spine is defined once, canonically, in funnel-builder Core Principles ("The Persuasive Spine and Entry Point" in `../SKILL.md`); this brief does not restate its order. Field 17 orders the section outline along that canonical spine, and the fine-grained close follows funnel-builder Section 9's close architecture. Market awareness sets WHERE you enter it; format sets how fast you move through it. The Schwartz Structural Layer (gated) weights which sections carry the most words; the spine and the weighting compose without conflict.
- The brief's emotional through-line (fields 4, 5, and 12) serves the angle card's **Core Feeling** - the one core feeling the funnel serves, whose enum canonical source is `../../copywriting-guide/SKILL.md` §8.7 The Five Core Feelings Library. The card's **Lead Emotion** (its opening trigger) sets the entry beat of fields 4-5; Core Feeling is the served feeling the whole brief holds to. This does not add an 18th field: it maps the existing emotional fields onto the card's canonical feeling contract. If Core Feeling is absent on the card, apply the angle-card schema's missing-field precedence matrix (see `../../angle-roadmap/references/angle-card-schema.md` "Missing-field precedence").
