# Worked Examples

Four long-format static ads broken down beat-by-beat, mapped to the section structure (`section-structure.md`) and named patterns (`named-patterns.md`). Use as audit checklist when reviewing your own draft. If your draft fails a beat that one of these passes, that is the limiting factor.

The four examples cover the major POV variants from pattern 9 (Three-Lead Rule):
- Example 1 (Derila Ergo): family-member-of-sufferer POV, informal authority, health-supplement vertical
- Example 2 (Roan ROAN NAD+): first-person sufferer POV, formal credentialed authority, health-supplement vertical with spousal stakes
- Example 3 (PiPi Tea Sandra): first-person professional-with-experience POV, self-as-authority + observed-discovery trigger, health-supplement vertical with patient-network validation
- Example 4 (ValueScout): first-person sufferer POV in non-medical context, self-as-authority + referenced-content discovery trigger, information-arbitrage vertical with information-asymmetry villain

This file complements `rosabella-pattern-summary.md`. The pattern summary is variant-level meta-analysis (14 Rosabella variants, which patterns each validates). This file is beat-level breakdown (4 ads, what each beat does and why). Different jobs, kept in separate files.

## How to use this file

1. Pick the example whose lead style and vertical most closely match your angle:
   - Family-member-of-sufferer POV → Example 1 (Derila Ergo)
   - First-person sufferer in health/physical-suffering context → Example 2 (Roan)
   - First-person professional-with-experience → Example 3 (PiPi Tea Sandra)
   - First-person sufferer in non-medical context → Example 4 (ValueScout)
2. Read the matching example end-to-end before drafting.
3. After drafting, run the audit checklist at the end of this file against your draft.
4. If your draft fails 3+ checks, return to the example for the specific beat that failed.

The breakdowns reference the existing 10 named patterns by number. Pattern definitions live in `named-patterns.md`; this file does not redefine them.

## Example 1: Derila Ergo (Sleep Pillow)

Twelve-beat breakdown. The ad ran ~3,400 words of primary text; below captures the structural shape, not verbatim copy.

### Beat 1: Pattern Interrupt Hook

**What they wrote (paraphrased, under 15 words):** A daughter's recollection of a 2 AM moment standing in a doorway watching her father struggle to breathe.
**What they did:** Open mid-scene at the worst moment. Identity marker (daughter) inside first 6 words; info gap (what was happening / what changed) implicit.
**Why it works:** Drops the reader inside the situation rather than building up to it. The 2 AM specificity passes the time-anchor test from word one.
**Map:** Hook + headline section in `section-structure.md`. Named-patterns.md pattern 1 (Desperation Frame) opener. Hook quality test from `self-test-checklist.md` item 3.

### Beat 2: Before & Breaking Point (merged)

**What they wrote:** The before-state of her father's life (active, present, the kind of grandfather who wrestled with the kids on the floor) collapsed into a single specific scene where he could not climb a single set of stairs without stopping twice.
**What they did:** Antecedent story compressed by merging "before" and "breaking point" into one beat. The contrast is the agitation engine.
**Why it works:** The reader doesn't need a long backstory. One vivid before-scene plus one collapsed-version scene does the same identification work as four pages of exposition.
**Map:** Identification opener + Antecedent / catalyst story + POV anchor + decision moment, all telescoped into ~400 words rather than ~1,400. Pattern 3 (Identification Before Mechanism) is doing the work; the failed-solution stack hasn't started yet.

### Beat 3: Failed Attempts

**What they wrote:** Itemized list with brands, prices, and durations. CPAP machine ($800, returned after 11 weeks). Mouthguard ($400, kept making him gag). Smart sleep tracker ($200, told him things he already knew). Doctor visits with normal bloodwork.
**What they did:** Specificity. Every failed attempt has a brand or specific descriptor, a dollar amount, a duration. No "I tried everything"; everything is named.
**Why it works:** The reader pattern-matches their own failed attempts. The deeper the specificity, the more matches the reader makes; the more matches, the higher their investment in the protagonist's eventual discovery.
**Map:** Failed-solution stack section. Validates the deep-specificity stack pattern (also seen in Rosabella variants 6, 9, 10).

### Beat 4: The Mechanism (Henrik dialogue)

**What they wrote:** The protagonist's stay at a guesthouse during an unrelated trip. Henrik, the guesthouse owner, asks why she keeps stepping outside to take phone calls in the middle of the night. She explains. Henrik tells her his own father had the same problem. The conversation moves through the mechanism in 4 short exchanges, with Henrik pointing at things (a pillow, a window, a bottle of olive oil) as he explains.
**What they did:** Mechanism reveal as dialogue with informal authority. Henrik is a layperson, not a doctor. The mechanism is delivered as a quote from him, with physical gestures breaking the information density.
**Why it works:** Authority surprise outperforms authority endorsement. A guesthouse owner explaining cardiovascular biology lands differently from a doctor explaining the same thing; the disproportion of the source signals that the information is real and not a sales pitch.
**Map:** Mechanism reveal section. Named-patterns.md pattern 4 (Three-Stage Mechanism Explanation) Stages 1-3, delivered through the pattern 4 dialogue framework subsection (informal authority variant). Pattern 8 (Authority Hook Patterns) "Classic" with informal-authority framing.

### Beat 5: The Discovery

**What they wrote:** The protagonist's reaction to Henrik's explanation. She asks him to repeat one specific phrase three times before it clicks. The relief and recognition arrive in two sentences. She writes the phrase down on a hotel napkin.
**What they did:** The discovery is small, narrow, concrete (a single phrase). Not a general epiphany.
**Why it works:** Specific discoveries land harder than general ones. A phrase the protagonist had to look up multiple times signals real difficulty and earned understanding.
**Map:** Mechanism reveal continuation, transitioning toward product introduction. Named-patterns.md pattern 2 (Discovery Story).

### Beat 6: The Proof Stack

**What they wrote:** Three things that happened in the weeks after the conversation. Her father's snoring stopped on day 4. The sleep tracker she had returned (because it told her things she already knew) was now telling her useful things again. Her mother stopped sleeping in the guest room.
**What they did:** Time-anchored, specific, woven through narrative rather than as a separate "results" section. Each proof point ties to a named relationship or named object from earlier in the body.
**Why it works:** The reader has already invested in the daughter / father / mother dynamic; the proof points land as story progression, not as testimonial.
**Map:** Results timeline, woven through narrative rather than separate. Named-patterns.md pattern 6 (Time-Delay Anchoring).

### Beat 7: Product Reveal

**What they wrote:** A single sentence. The protagonist names the brand and the specific product, in passing, as the product she sourced after Henrik's conversation, after testing several alternatives.
**What they did:** Anticlimactic. The story did the selling; the product reveal is one casual sentence.
**Why it works:** By this point in the body, the reader has accepted the mechanism, recognized themselves in the antecedent, and watched the proof stack arrive. The product is the natural resolution; an aggressive product reveal here would break the spell.
**Map:** Product introduction section. Per the 75% rule (`section-structure.md` Section-Level Writing Notes), the product name appears at approximately 77% of body word count.

### Beat 8: Skepticism & Try

**What they wrote:** A short paragraph naming the protagonist's own initial skepticism. She had returned three sleep products in the past year; she expected to return this one too. She kept the receipt.
**What they did:** Failed-solution stack revisited via humor and self-deprecation. Pre-empts the reader's own "I've been burned before" objection.
**Why it works:** The reader sees their own skepticism reflected. The protagonist's willingness to name her own pattern of returns signals that she isn't blindly endorsing the product.
**Map:** Differentiation block (informal). The "vs the cheap alternatives" work is being done implicitly through the protagonist's previous returns.

### Beat 9: The After

**What they wrote:** A specific scene from 8 weeks later. The father wrestled with the grandkids on the floor for the first time in 4 years. The mother cried. The protagonist describes hanging back to give them the moment.
**What they did:** Mirror moment. Every wound from the identification opener is healed in the After. The "active, present grandfather" image from beat 2 returns, restored.
**Why it works:** The 1:1 mirror between identification and After is the structural payoff of the whole ad. The reader doesn't have to be told the product worked; they see the same image with the wound removed.
**Map:** Durability proof + Mirror moment sections. Named-patterns.md pattern 7 (Five Core Feelings) "identity" feeling sustained from beat 1 through beat 9.

### Beat 10: Offer Stack

**What they wrote:** The protagonist describes that she ordered three more for her father (one for travel, one for the guest room, one as a backup). She mentions the brand offered her a multi-pack discount; she took it.
**What they did:** Differentiation vs others (closing) and risk reversal demonstrated through behavior, not stated. The protagonist's actions are the offer reveal; she's not pitching, she's describing what she did.
**Why it works:** Behavior-based offer reveals are higher-conversion than direct offer pitches. The reader infers the offer's value from the protagonist's choice, not from a sales claim.
**Map:** Differentiation vs others (closing) + Risk reversal sections.

### Beat 11: Urgency & Close

**What they wrote:** A two-sentence aside about the brand running low on inventory; the protagonist had to wait 9 days for her last order. She suggests not waiting.
**What they did:** Urgency framed as a specific past inconvenience (her own 9-day wait). Crossroads framing implicit (she chose to wait; she's recommending the reader skip the wait).
**Why it works:** Specific urgency outperforms generic urgency. "I waited 9 days" is verifiable in the reader's mind; "limited stock" is not.
**Map:** Crossroads framing + CTA section.

### Beat 12: PS

**What they wrote:** A callback to Henrik. The protagonist mentions that Henrik's wife emailed her last week; her own mother had just started sleeping through the night.
**What they did:** PS as a callback to the antecedent character (Henrik), introducing a new but parallel data point (Henrik's wife / mother).
**Why it works:** The PS earns the highest engagement of the body; the reader who scrolled to the bottom for the offer reads the PS and gets pulled back into the story.
**Map:** PS section. Pattern from Rosabella variants 1, 5, 11, 13 (callback-to-story PSs).

### Ten key learnings from the Derila breakdown

1. Open loop intensity at life-or-death level. The hook drops the reader inside the worst moment.
2. Drop the reader INTO the worst moment, do not build up to it.
3. Child witness as nuclear weapon. The daughter's POV multiplies the emotional weight.
4. Failed attempts with exact dollar amounts and the avatar's voice.
5. Mechanism delivered through dialogue with authority, not exposition.
6. Discovery feels accidental (a guesthouse stay during an unrelated trip).
7. Authority surprise outperforms authority endorsement (guesthouse owner, not doctor).
8. Mirror every pain point in the After. The structural payoff is the 1:1 healing.
9. Product reveal in one casual sentence. The story did the selling.
10. Close like a friend giving a tip. "By the way" energy, not "BUY NOW".

## Example 2: Roan ROAN NAD+ Men's Complex (Prostate / Nocturia Supplement)

Eleven-beat breakdown. The ad ran ~1,400 words of primary text; this is a shorter ad than Derila, demonstrating that the framework works at multiple lengths.

### Lead style contrast with Derila

- Derila: family-member-of-sufferer (daughter narrating her father's situation, third-degree perspective looking inward at family).
- Roan: first-person sufferer (Larry IS the protagonist, narrating his own experience). The reader's identification is direct, not mediated through a family relationship.

This is the first-person sufferer POV from named-patterns.md pattern 9 (Three-Lead Rule), which the prior 2-example set did not demonstrate.

### Authority type contrast with Derila

- Derila: informal authority (Henrik, the guesthouse owner - layperson with personal experience).
- Roan: formal credentialed authority (Jean-Claude, retired pharmacist from Lyon, France - 35 years of pharmacy practice, named European pharmaceutical brands and clinical trial counts).

Both deliver the mechanism through dialogue per pattern 4 dialogue framework, but the authority surface differs. Roan demonstrates the formal-credentialed variant the prior set did not.

### Beat 1: Pattern Interrupt Hook

**What they wrote (paraphrased, under 15 words):** A specific dollar amount lost over seven years on American prostate treatments, contrasted against a $40 European pharmacy answer.
**What they did:** Open with a financial loss frame, specific number ($3,200), specific time anchor (seven years), specific contrast ($40). Identity marker (American prostate treatments) inside first 10 words. Information gap implicit: who told him, what they knew, why American doctors didn't.
**Why it works:** The financial loss specificity passes the time-anchor test from word one. The dollar contrast ($3,200 vs $40) is verifiable, vivid, and immediately frames the rest of the ad as a redress-of-injustice story rather than a discovery story.
**Map:** Hook + headline section. Named-patterns.md pattern 1 (Desperation Frame Opener) with a financial/identity loss variant rather than acute physical suffering. Hook quality test from `self-test-checklist.md`.

### Beat 2: Identity Reveal + Stakes Anchor

**What they wrote:** Larry Ostrowski. 67. Retired machinist out of Grand Rapids, Michigan. Wife Bev. Sold the house in 2022, bought a fifth-wheel RV. Full-time RVers. Plan was to travel the country, hike every national park. Then the prostate had other plans.
**What they did:** Identity specificity in 5 sentences. Name, age, occupation, geographic origin, current life setup (fifth-wheel RV), stated aspiration (national parks). The protagonist is fully specific before any problem detail lands.
**Why it works:** Pattern 3 (Identification Before Mechanism) is doing the work; the reader feels the man and the life before the symptom enters. The RV setup is doing structural double-duty: it's identity, AND it's the constraint that makes the bathroom problem unbearable (six feet from the bed, whole rig moves).
**Map:** Identification opener section. Pattern 9 lead variant 1 (first-person sufferer) executed with full demographic specificity.

### Beat 3: The Specific Lived Worst-Moment Scene

**What they wrote:** BPH at 58. Up four times a night by 62. Five times by 65. Bathroom is six feet from the bed in a 38-foot trailer. Every trip, the whole rig moves. Bev wakes every single time. She started wearing earplugs. They didn't help. You can't earplug away a rocking trailer. Then: Bev's quote at the breaking point ("Larry, I love the travel life. But I can't live in a tin can with a man who uses the bathroom five times a night. I haven't slept in three years.").
**What they did:** The breaking-point scene is grounded in physical specificity (38-foot trailer, six feet, rocking rig), relational specificity (Bev's quote in direct dialogue), and time specificity (three years of her not sleeping).
**Why it works:** The bathroom problem is universally unsympathetic (every man has prostate jokes). The trailer specificity makes it unique. Bev's quote externalizes the stakes from physical discomfort to relationship preservation. The "three years" data point reveals the sufferer's wife has been suffering too - a relational stakes amplifier the prior worked-examples set did not demonstrate.
**Map:** Antecedent / catalyst story + Decision moment, merged. Pattern 3 (Identification Before Mechanism) with spousal-stakes amplification.

### Beat 4: Failed-Solution Stack with Itemized Dollar Specificity

**What they wrote:** Flomax (14 months, side effects detailed - dizziness in Sedona RV park, retrograde ejaculation). Finasteride (filled the prescription, read the warnings, poured the pills into the trash). Saw Palmetto three brands (11 months combined). Super Beta Prostate (4 months). ProstaGenix (3 months, "expensive nothing"). An Amazon supplement with 8,000 reviews (2 months). Total: $3,200 over seven years. Total improvement: zero.
**What they did:** Six named alternatives with durations, dollar specificity at the stack level ($3,200), and one near-miss (finasteride pills poured into trash on reading the side-effect label - a behavioral detail that lands).
**Why it works:** The reader pattern-matches their own failed-attempt history. Every man over 60 with BPH has tried at least 2-3 of these named alternatives. Specificity earns the protagonist's authority - he didn't dabble, he was thorough.
**Map:** Failed-solution stack section. Validates the deep-specificity stack pattern (also seen in Derila beat 3 and Rosabella variants 6, 9, 10).

### Beat 5: The Authority Encounter

**What they wrote:** Last January, parked at an RV resort outside Sarasota, Florida. Snowbird season. Larry struck up a poolside conversation with a man named Jean-Claude. French-Canadian, originally from Lyon, France. 73 years old. Retired pharmacist. Larry mentions the nocturia. Jean-Claude looks at him with "genuine confusion" and asks "Why are you not taking the plant extracts?"
**What they did:** The authority encounter is grounded in specific setting (Sarasota RV resort, snowbird season, poolside), specific authority (named, aged, regionally specific French pharmacist), and specific reaction (confusion, not condescension). The authority's confusion is the pattern interrupt - he expects Larry to be on the European protocol and isn't.
**Why it works:** Authority surprise outperforms authority endorsement. Jean-Claude isn't selling Larry on anything; he's confused that Larry doesn't already know. The confusion frame positions the information as obvious-to-Europeans rather than special-knowledge.
**Map:** Mechanism reveal section, dialogue opener. Pattern 4 (Three-Stage Mechanism Explanation) Stage 1 (mechanism naming) delivered through pattern 4 dialogue framework (formal-credentialed authority variant). Pattern 8 (Authority Hook Patterns) "Doctor's Surprise" with credentialed-pharmacist framing.

### Beat 6: The Mechanism in Dialogue

**What they wrote:** Jean-Claude explains: not the American Saw Palmetto from the drugstore. The pharmaceutical preparation - Permixon (Saw Palmetto extract), Tadenan (Pygeum africanum extract). Prescription medications in France since the 1970s. Over 20 clinical trials on Permixon alone. The extract is standardized. The dose is clinical. Combined with other botanicals - Pygeum, ortie racine (Nettle Root). Multiple pathways. Then on delivery: the ideal delivery for lipophilic botanical extracts is sublingual. Under the tongue. Bypass the stomach. Bypass the liver. Direct absorption into blood. "In France, we understand this. In America, you put everything in a horse capsule and wonder why it doesn't work."
**What they did:** Mechanism in 4 dialogue beats, each grounded in a specific named element (Permixon, Tadenan, ortie racine, sublingual delivery). The mechanism is delivered as professional opinion with the kind of cross-cultural shaming ("horse capsule") that signals authentic professional frustration.
**Why it works:** Three-stage mechanism explanation (pattern 4): Stage 1 names the elements; Stage 2 explains why they work together (multiple pathways); Stage 3 differentiates with specifics (standardized extract, clinical dose, sublingual delivery, 20 clinical trials on Permixon alone). The numerical specificity (20 trials) plus the named pharmaceutical formulations (Permixon, Tadenan) creates a verifiable claim surface that the reader can search later. The reader doesn't have to verify in the moment - the specificity does the trust work.
**Map:** Mechanism reveal section. Named-patterns.md pattern 4 (Three-Stage Mechanism Explanation) all three stages, delivered through pattern 4 dialogue framework (formal-credentialed authority variant).

### Beat 7: The Discovery Action

**What they wrote:** Larry and Jean-Claude talk for two hours that afternoon. Jean-Claude draws diagrams in the condensation on his beer glass. He explains first-pass metabolism. Why a 60-year-old digestive system destroys capsule supplements. Why multi-botanical matters. Before Larry leaves the pool, Jean-Claude writes on a napkin: "Find a sublingual prostate formula. Multiple botanicals. Not capsules. And include zinc."
**What they did:** The discovery is concrete (a napkin with four written-down requirements). Not a general epiphany. The transition from authority dialogue to action is mediated by a physical artifact (the napkin) that the protagonist can act on.
**Why it works:** Specific discoveries land harder than general ones. A napkin with four bullet points signals real difficulty in deciding what to buy - it's not "Jean-Claude told me about supplements," it's "Jean-Claude wrote down exactly what to look for."
**Map:** Mechanism reveal continuation, transitioning toward product introduction. Named-patterns.md pattern 2 (Discovery Story Format) with a physical-artifact discovery anchor.

### Beat 8: Product Introduction (Pattern 4 Stage 4 - Preparation Differentiation)

**What they wrote:** Larry searches for three hours that night. Finds ROAN NAD+ Men's Complex. Sublingual dropper. Saw Palmetto, Pygeum, Nettle Root, Zinc, NAD+, Turmeric. Under the tongue. $40. 30-day guarantee. Shows Jean-Claude the ingredient list the next morning. Jean-Claude reads it carefully: "This is close to what I would have recommended in my pharmacy. The NAD+ is a good addition - your prostate cells need energy to repair. Try it."
**What they did:** Product reveal in one sentence followed by ingredient list and authority validation. The mechanism has been established; the product is the natural conclusion. Sublingual delivery and multi-botanical formulation match the napkin's four requirements explicitly.
**Why it works:** Pattern 4 Stage 4 (Preparation Differentiation) is the key teaching point. The mechanism here (botanical prostate extracts) is generic - anyone can buy Saw Palmetto. The product differentiation moves entirely to PREPARATION (sublingual vs capsule, multi-botanical vs single, clinical dose vs filler, pharmaceutical-grade extract vs commodity). This is the differentiation strategy when the mechanism itself is widely available.
**Map:** Product introduction section. Per the 75% rule (`section-structure.md` Section-Level Writing Notes), the product name appears at approximately 73% of body word count (slightly earlier than Derila's 77% because the ad is shorter). Pattern 4 Stage 4 (Preparation Differentiation) - the differentiation moves from mechanism to preparation when the mechanism is widely available.

### Beat 9: Time-Anchored Results

**What they wrote:** "Six weeks later, I walked back to that pool and found Jean-Claude. 'Three trips down to one,' I said. 'Most nights. Some nights zero.' He nodded. Not surprised. Not even a little. 'Of course,' he said. 'These compounds work. They have always worked. Your country simply refuses to use them.'"
**What they did:** Specific time anchor (six weeks). Specific quantified result (three trips → one, some nights zero). Authority validation revisited (Jean-Claude unsurprised, confirming the mechanism).
**Why it works:** Pattern 6 (Time-Delay Anchoring) executed cleanly. Six weeks is a believable timeframe; the result is modest (one trip down to one, not zero) and verifiable.
**Map:** Results timeline. Named-patterns.md pattern 6 (Time-Delay Anchoring).

### Beat 10: The After (Spousal-Stakes Resolution)

**What they wrote:** "Bev hasn't worn earplugs in four weeks. The trailer doesn't rock five times a night anymore. We drove from Sarasota to Asheville last month - nine hours over two days - and I stopped twice. Twice. Bev had a rest stop map with 14 locations plotted. We used two. She said 'Larry Ostrowski, I feel like I got my husband back.' We're keeping the RV. We're heading to Yellowstone in June. And I'm sleeping through the night in a 38-foot tin can, which is something I couldn't do in a 2,400-square-foot house six months ago."
**What they did:** Mirror moment with extreme specificity. Every wound from beat 3 is healed: Bev no earplugs (vs earplugs that didn't work), trailer not rocking (vs rocking every trip), 2 stops vs 14 plotted (vs the 5 trips a night), and the explicit return-to-RV-plan (vs almost selling the RV).
**Why it works:** The 1:1 mirror between identification opener and After is the structural payoff. The reader doesn't have to be told the product worked; they see the same RV with the wound removed. The "2 vs 14" stop count is a verifiable metric the reader's brain remembers.
**Map:** Durability proof + Mirror moment sections. Named-patterns.md pattern 7 (Five Core Feelings) "vindication" feeling sustained from beat 1 through beat 10.

### Beat 11: PS with Authority Quote Callback

**What they wrote:** Jean-Claude is given a final word: "Tell the American men that in Europe, we consider it malpractice to prescribe finasteride before trying phytotherapy. Your doctors are skipping the safest and most effective first step. That is not medicine. That is marketing." Larry adds: "He said it with the kind of quiet anger that only a man who spent 35 years in medicine can carry. He's right. And now you know what he knows. $40. No prescription needed. No side effects. No surgeon. Just the medicine that Europe has been using for half a century."
**What they did:** PS as authority-quote callback. Jean-Claude's quote raises the stakes from "this worked for me" to "the American medical system is malpracticing." The PS earns the highest engagement of the body; the reader who scrolled to the bottom for the offer gets pulled back into the ideological argument.
**Why it works:** Pattern 8 (Authority Hook Patterns) "Doctor's Skepticism" frame inverted - the authority figure is the foreign-credentialed pharmacist accusing American medicine of malpractice. The frame works because (a) it has a specific named authority backing it, (b) the accusation is specific (skipping phytotherapy before finasteride), and (c) the protagonist's own experience validates the accusation. The reader's relationship with their own American doctor is implicitly recast.
**Map:** PS section. Pattern from Rosabella variants 1, 5, 11, 13 (callback-to-story PSs), executed here with authority-quote escalation.

### Ten key learnings from the Roan breakdown

1. First-person sufferer POV works at full strength when identity specificity lands in the first 5 sentences.
2. Spousal stakes amplify physical-suffering ads (Bev's three years of not sleeping recasts a "prostate problem" as a relationship-preservation problem).
3. Formal credentialed authority works through specific named formulations (Permixon, Tadenan, ortie racine) rather than credentials alone.
4. Pattern 4 Stage 4 (Preparation Differentiation) is the right differentiation strategy when the mechanism itself is generic (Saw Palmetto, hibiscus, omega-3, etc.).
5. Sublingual-vs-capsule frame is a specific preparation differentiator that works because it's mechanistically explainable (first-pass metabolism, lipophilic extract absorption).
6. Authority shaming ("In America, you put everything in a horse capsule") signals authentic professional frustration rather than salesmanship.
7. Physical artifact discovery anchors (napkin with four bullets) are more believable than general epiphanies.
8. The After section's specificity (14 plotted rest stops, used 2) gives the reader a verifiable metric to remember.
9. PS authority-quote callback escalates the ad from personal-experience to ideological-critique without losing the personal anchor.
10. Same architectural shape as Derila (~12 beats), shorter body (~1,400 words vs ~3,400). The framework scales across lengths.

## Example 3: PiPi Tea (Hibiscus Tea for Kidney Function)

Ten-beat breakdown. Variant #3 of 21 in the PiPi Tea ad rotation. The ad ran ~1,500 words. Demonstrates the professional-with-experience lead POV from pattern 9, the observed-discovery trigger as a pattern 2 variant, and pattern 4 Stage 4 (Preparation Differentiation) for generic-mechanism categories.

### Lead style contrast with Derila and Roan

- Derila: family-member-of-sufferer (daughter narrating father's situation).
- Roan: first-person sufferer (Larry narrating his own situation).
- PiPi Tea: first-person professional-with-experience (Sandra is a nurse who has the condition she treats).

This is the third lead variant from pattern 9 (Three-Lead Rule) - the professional-with-experience POV, where the narrator's credentials and personal experience reinforce each other. The prior 2-example set did not demonstrate this.

### Authority and discovery trigger contrast

- Derila and Roan: discovery triggered by direct authority dialogue (Henrik, Jean-Claude).
- PiPi Tea: discovery triggered by **observed patient outcome**. Sandra notices one of her regular patients (with stage 3 kidney disease) is "lighter, different" at a routine appointment. The patient mentions hibiscus tea. Sandra investigates her own situation as a result.

This is a pattern 2 (Discovery Story Format) variant where the discovery trigger is observed-outcome-in-a-third-party rather than direct authority dialogue. The discovery still feels accidental, but the trigger surface is different. The prior 2-example set did not demonstrate this.

### Vertical and demographic contrast

- Derila: older male sufferer, family-discovers-for-sufferer angle, health supplement.
- Roan: older male sufferer with spousal stakes, first-person, health supplement.
- PiPi Tea: middle-aged female professional sufferer, first-person, health supplement.

All three remain in the health-supplement vertical (necessary for the Derila comparison axis) but the demographic and POV variation is broad.

### Compliance Redline (READ BEFORE USING THIS EXAMPLE AS A TEMPLATE)

This worked example demonstrates real structural moves that produce high-converting copy in the supplement vertical. The structural moves are reusable: professional-with-experience POV (pattern 9), observed-discovery trigger (pattern 2 variant), preparation differentiation (pattern 4 stage 4), professional-humility close.

The surface elements are NOT reusable as written. This ad uses specific clinical claims, named conditions, and patient outcomes that would fail FDA/FTC structure-function claim requirements AND Meta ad platform review:

- **Clinical metric claims** (eGFR 42 → 58 in three months): do not reproduce specific clinical-test outcomes.
- **Named disease treatment** (Stage 3 kidney disease, kidney function "stable"): do not claim a supplement treats or stabilizes a diagnosed condition.
- **Aggregated patient outcomes** ("I've told five of my patients... every single one of them has come back and said their function is finally stable"): do not aggregate clinical outcomes across patients as evidence.
- **Standard-of-care critique** (dialysis pivot scene): be cautious framing a supplement as an alternative to medical treatment for a diagnosed condition.

When adapting this example to compliant copy: replace the clinical specifics with structure-function language ("supports kidney health," "supports normal filtration") and remove condition-specific framing (remove "Stage 3 kidney disease," replace with "kidney health concerns" or similar). Replace eGFR metric with energy / fatigue / sleep quality outcomes that don't reference clinical tests. Keep the structural moves (professional-with-experience narrator, observed-outcome discovery, preparation differentiation, professional-humility close) intact.

The beats below describe what the ad actually says. The beat descriptions reference the non-compliant surface elements because that is what is in the source ad. ### Beat 1: Identity-Anchored Authority Hook

**What they wrote (paraphrased, under 15 words):** A nurse with Stage 3 kidney disease who tried everything, sharing what actually worked.
**What they did:** Identity marker (nurse), credential anchor (38 years of practice), specific condition (Stage 3 kidney disease), failed-everything stake, promise of what worked. Authority and personal experience fused in one sentence.
**Why it works:** Authority + sufferer fusion is harder for the reader to dismiss than either alone. A nurse alone could be selling something; a sufferer alone might not understand the science; a nurse-who-suffers has both credibility and stakes.
**Map:** Hook + headline section. Named-patterns.md pattern 1 (Desperation Frame Opener) executed with pattern 9 professional-with-experience lead.

### Beat 2: Professional Context + Personal Transition

**What they wrote:** 38 years as a nurse. Has seen kidney disease in patients - foamy urine, burning when peeing, swollen ankles, 3-4 bathroom trips a night. Lists what she's seen patients say. Then: three years ago, it happened to her. Years of long shifts and not taking care of herself caught up. Stage 3 kidney disease.
**What they did:** Establishes Sandra's professional authority through specific clinical symptom vocabulary (foamy urine, swollen ankles, etc.) - things only someone with clinical experience says casually. Then transitions to first-person sufferer.
**Why it works:** Pattern 3 (Identification Before Mechanism) executed by establishing professional identity FIRST (so credentials are felt), THEN revealing personal stake (so the reader connects). The reverse order would feel like a sales pitch ("I have this condition, btw I'm a nurse"); this order feels like a colleague sharing.
**Map:** Identification opener + Antecedent / catalyst story telescoped. Pattern 9 lead variant 3 (professional-with-experience) executed with sequenced authority-then-stakes.

### Beat 3: Lived Worst-Moment Scene

**What they wrote:** Tired after lunch. Swelling. Sudden exhaustion and brain fog. By end of shift, couldn't think straight. Worst part: nighttime. Wake up needing to pee. Again. And again. Three, sometimes four times a night. Exhausted and thirsty no matter how much water she drank. Half the night trying to get back to sleep.
**What they did:** Specific symptom progression with time anchors (after lunch, end of shift, nighttime). Sensory specificity (brain fog, exhaustion-and-thirst contradiction). Then explicit professional reflection: "Now I was the one living with it. And let me tell you, every patient was right. It's a living hell."
**Why it works:** The professional-experience-now-personal frame is doing double work - Sandra validates her patients' descriptions through her own experience, which makes her later product recommendation feel like clinical observation rather than sales pitch.
**Map:** Decision moment + Antecedent story continuation. Pattern 3 (Identification Before Mechanism) with professional-reflection anchoring.

### Beat 4: The Dialysis Pivot (Stake Amplifier)

**What they wrote:** Doctor offered dialysis preparation. Sandra smiled politely, said she'd think about it. Then revealed: she's watched too many patients struggle with the process. Fistula surgery taking months to heal. Three-times-a-week schedule taking over their lives. Kidney function still declining. "Most of them tell me it barely keeps them stable. I wasn't about to trade one nightmare for another."
**What they did:** Stake amplification through professional observation. The dialysis pivot raises the stakes beyond Sandra's current suffering to a worse future. The "watched too many patients" framing positions Sandra as someone who knows what dialysis actually means, not just what doctors tell patients.
**Why it works:** The implicit critique of standard medical advice ("doctors offer dialysis prep") delivered from inside the medical system carries more weight than an outsider's anti-medical posture. Sandra isn't anti-doctor; she's a nurse refusing to undergo a procedure she has watched fail.
**Map:** Decision moment escalation. Named-patterns.md pattern 8 (Authority Hook Patterns) "Doctor's Skepticism" inverted - Sandra is the authority figure being skeptical of her own profession's recommendation.

### Beat 5: Failed-Solution Stack

**What they wrote:** "I just dealt with it. Cranberry supplements. Kidney support blends. Cutting protein completely. Nothing really worked, but what else was I supposed to do?"
**What they did:** Failed-solution stack compressed to four items in two sentences. Lower specificity than Derila or Roan because the stack is short, but the rhetorical question "what else was I supposed to do?" externalizes the failure to the available options, not Sandra's effort.
**Why it works:** Sandra's professional credibility allows for a shorter failed-solution stack - the reader trusts that she would have tried the obvious options. A non-professional protagonist would need a longer stack to establish thoroughness.
**Map:** Failed-solution stack section. Compressed variant when narrator credibility allows.

### Beat 6: Observed Discovery Trigger

**What they wrote:** Two months ago, one of Sandra's regular patients came in for an appointment. Stage 3 kidney disease, declining function for years, struggled with it for over a decade. Always tried everything, nothing gave her more than a few points of stable eGFR. But this time she seemed different. Lighter somehow. Sandra asked how she was managing. The patient said her kidney function actually wasn't declining anymore. She'd found this tea - hibiscus tea - with natural antioxidants. Drinking it every morning. Sleeping through the night.
**What they did:** The discovery is triggered by OBSERVING a third party (the patient), not by direct conversation with an authority figure. Sandra is the observer; the patient is the unwitting authority. Sandra's reaction is professional skepticism ("I've heard every miracle cure story you can imagine") which she names explicitly - this preempts the reader's identical skepticism.
**Why it works:** Pattern 2 (Discovery Story Format) variant: discovery via observed-outcome rather than authority-dialogue. The trigger is the noticeable change in someone else's body, which the protagonist witnesses with professional attention. This is a different discovery trigger surface than the prior 2-example set demonstrated.
**Map:** Mechanism reveal opener via observed-outcome trigger. Named-patterns.md pattern 2 (Discovery Story Format) with observed-outcome trigger variant.

### Beat 7: Mechanism Self-Research

**What they wrote:** That night, Sandra's kidneys acting up again. Couldn't sleep. Looked it up. Turns out, the antioxidants in hibiscus tea - anthocyanins, polyphenols, flavonoids, organic acids - restore kidney cells' ability to filter toxins properly. Doesn't force kidneys to work harder like treatments do. Reduces inflammation. Supports nephrons naturally. "The science actually made sense."
**What they did:** Mechanism delivered through Sandra's own research, not through authority dialogue. Named specific compounds (anthocyanins, polyphenols, flavonoids, organic acids), explained the mechanism in clinical-but-plain language (restore filtration, reduce inflammation, support nephrons), and explicitly validated as a professional ("the science actually made sense").
**Why it works:** Three-stage mechanism explanation (pattern 4) compressed because the narrator's credentials carry the proof weight. Stage 1 (named compounds), Stage 2 (mechanism explained in clinical-plain language), Stage 3 (professional validation as a substitute for external proof points). Pattern 4 dialogue framework is not used here - the dialogue is internal (self-research) rather than external.
**Map:** Mechanism reveal section. Named-patterns.md pattern 4 (Three-Stage Mechanism Explanation) all three stages, delivered through internal-research rather than authority dialogue. This is the variant when the narrator IS the authority.

### Beat 8: Pattern 4 Stage 4 - The Generic-vs-Specific Preparation Reveal

**What they wrote:** Sandra orders hibiscus tea. After a week, eGFR improves slightly (42 → 45). Better but not life-changing. Orders a different brand. Two weeks later, numbers about the same (46 in the mornings). Still too low. Getting frustrated. Goes back, reads more carefully. People weren't just saying "hibiscus tea." They were getting specific. One person tried three different brands before finding one that worked. Another mentioned most hibiscus tea online is "flower dust" - broken leftover scraps without healing compounds. Lists what actually works: whole flower (not dust), grown above 4,500 feet (higher altitude = more potent antioxidants), hand-picked at peak harvest, traditionally processed, organic. Sandra orders PiPi Tea. First morning, energy stays steady. That night, sleeps through. Next morning, stunned. By end of first week, eGFR at 54 (not 46).
**What they did:** Pattern 4 Stage 4 (Preparation Differentiation) executed in extended form. The mechanism (hibiscus tea antioxidants) is generic and widely available. The product differentiation moves entirely to PREPARATION specifics: whole flower vs dust, altitude, hand-picked, traditional processing, organic. Each preparation specific is mechanistically explainable (higher altitude → more antioxidant content; whole flower → preserved compounds; etc.).
**Why it works:** This is the key teaching point of the Sandra ad. When the mechanism is so generic that any reader could buy "hibiscus tea" from a hundred sources, the entire product differentiation must move to preparation/source/format. Pattern 4 Stage 4 names what makes THIS preparation different. The result evidence (eGFR 42 → 54) is tied directly to the preparation switch, not to the mechanism class.
**Map:** Product introduction section with extended differentiation. Pattern 4 Stage 4 (Preparation Differentiation) executed in long-form. Product reveal lands at approximately 65% of body word count (earlier than Derila or Roan because the ad's pivot point is the preparation switch, not the mechanism reveal).

### Beat 9: Time-Anchored Results with Verifiable Metric

**What they wrote:** "It's been two months now. I sleep through the night. Every night. The foaming is gone. The burning is gone. Sometimes I get a tiny bit of fatigue after a really long shift, but it's nothing. Nothing compared to what it was." Then later: "I went from eGFR 42 to 58 in three months."
**What they did:** Time anchors throughout (two months ago, one week, end of first week, three months). Specific verifiable metric (eGFR 42 → 58). Modest qualifier on the after-state ("Sometimes I get a tiny bit of fatigue after a really long shift") that signals authenticity.
**Why it works:** Pattern 6 (Time-Delay Anchoring) executed cleanly. The eGFR metric is verifiable in a way that "I feel better" is not - the reader can search what eGFR means and what range is healthy. Sandra's professional credibility makes the metric land harder than the same metric from a non-professional narrator.
**Map:** Results timeline. Named-patterns.md pattern 6 (Time-Delay Anchoring) with verifiable clinical metric.

### Beat 10: Patient-Network Validation + Close

**What they wrote:** Saw her patient last week, thanked her. The patient smiled, said she was glad it helped. Sandra adds: "I've told five of my patients with kidney disease about PiPi Tea specifically. Every single one of them has come back and said their function is finally stable. One lady told me she slept through the night for the first time in three years." Then close: "I'm sharing this because I hope it can help someone else the way it helped me. After 38 years as a nurse, I thought I knew every option out there. Turns out, my patient knew something I didn't."
**What they did:** Validation through Sandra's professional network (5 patients). Each patient outcome is a small clinical anecdote that compounds the proof stack. Close frames the entire story as professional humility (a 38-year nurse learning from a patient) rather than salesmanship.
**Why it works:** The professional-humility frame ("my patient knew something I didn't") is structurally powerful because it inverts the expected authority dynamic. Sandra positions herself as a learner, not a seller. The reader can buy from a learner more easily than from a seller.
**Map:** Durability proof + Mirror moment + Close, merged. Named-patterns.md pattern 7 (Five Core Feelings) "vindication" feeling sustained, with professional-humility close variant.

### Ten key learnings from the PiPi Tea breakdown

1. Professional-with-experience lead POV (pattern 9 variant 3) works when professional credentials and personal stakes are sequenced: authority first, then personal reveal.
2. Observed-outcome discovery trigger (third party noticed to be improving) is a pattern 2 variant that doesn't require authority dialogue.
3. Pattern 4 Stage 4 (Preparation Differentiation) is essential when the mechanism itself is widely available (hibiscus tea, Saw Palmetto, omega-3 - anything commodity).
4. Failed-solution stacks can be compressed when narrator credentials carry implicit thoroughness.
5. Mechanism can be delivered through internal-research rather than authority-dialogue when the narrator IS the authority.
6. Inverted Doctor's Skepticism (the narrator is the authority figure being skeptical of her own profession's recommendation) lands harder than outsider critique.
7. Verifiable clinical metrics (eGFR 42 → 58) outperform feel-based result language.
8. Patient-network validation (5 patients improving) is the professional-context equivalent of the family-network validation in Derila.
9. Professional-humility close ("my patient knew something I didn't") inverts expected authority dynamics and reduces salesmanship feel.
10. Same architectural shape as Derila and Roan, but the product reveal lands earlier (~65%) because the pivot point is preparation-switch, not mechanism-reveal.

## Example 4: ValueScout (Reselling Pricing App)

Eleven-beat breakdown. The ad ran ~1,800 words. Demonstrates framework portability to a non-health vertical (information arbitrage / reselling), first-person sufferer in a non-medical context, and an information-asymmetry villain frame instead of a body-mechanism villain frame.

### Vertical contrast with other examples

- Derila, Roan, PiPi Tea: all health-supplement vertical (sleep aid, prostate, kidney). All physical-suffering narratives.
- ValueScout: information arbitrage / reselling. No physical suffering. The avatar's pain is decision-fatigue, regret cycles, and financial loss from underpricing items.

This is the first non-health-vertical worked example. The framework's structural shape carries across, but the surface texture (failed attempts, mechanism, alternative attack) is entirely different. Operators working on non-supplement brands have an example tuned to their tone calibration.

### Lead POV contrast

- ValueScout: first-person sufferer in a non-medical context. The protagonist is the seller herself, not someone discovering for a family member or a professional reflecting on patients.

The first-person sufferer POV (also seen in Roan) is reinforced here in a non-physical-suffering context, which expands the variant's demonstrated range.

### Villain frame contrast

- Health verticals: the villain is a body-mechanism (DHT, kidney filtration decline, inflammation, BPH) or an industry that ignores the body-mechanism.
- ValueScout: the villain is **information asymmetry**. Professional resellers have access to sold-price data; casual sellers see only asking-price data. The data exists; the casual seller is locked out of the room.

This is a paywalled-knowledge villain frame. The structural job (externalize blame from protagonist to external force) is the same; the surface is entirely different.

### Beat 1: Contrarian-Counsel Hook

**What they wrote (paraphrased, under 15 words):** Please stop trying to price your own stuff.
**What they did:** Contrarian open. The protagonist tells the reader to STOP doing the obvious thing the reader is currently doing. Identity marker (people who price their own stuff) inside the first 6 words. Information gap implicit (why stopping is the right move).
**Why it works:** Contrarian openers earn attention because they violate expectation. The reader's reflex is "what do you mean stop, I have to price my stuff to sell it" - and that reflex is the engagement.
**Map:** Hook + headline section. Named-patterns.md pattern 1 (Desperation Frame Opener) with a contrarian-counsel variant rather than direct desperation language. The desperation is implicit in the reader's recognized situation.

### Beat 2: Validation + Specific Lived Worst-Moment

**What they wrote:** "I know that sounds insane when you're staring at a closet full of things you've been meaning to sell." Then: "Because what you're doing right now - the eBay sold listings, the Google searches, the 'let me ask in the Reddit group' cycle - is like bailing water out of a sinking boat without plugging the hole." Then the protagonist's first item: a vintage Pyrex bowl listed on Facebook Marketplace for $15. Snapped within minutes. Felt like a sucker punch. Checked sold eBay listings the next morning. Same bowl. $48. $52. One for $61. "$40 left on the table on a single bowl."
**What they did:** Validation of the reader's current state (closet full of unsold items), then specific lived worst-moment (the Pyrex bowl underpricing). Specific dollar amount ($40 left on the table), specific identity marker (vintage Pyrex), specific platform (Facebook Marketplace).
**Why it works:** Pattern 3 (Identification Before Mechanism) executed without a physical-suffering scene. The financial-loss specificity ($40 on one bowl) is the equivalent of the trailer-rocking specificity in Roan. The reader pattern-matches her own underpriced sales.
**Map:** Identification opener with specific catalyst story (the bowl incident). Pattern 3 with financial-loss specificity.

### Beat 3: The Cycle Description

**What they wrote:** Detailed description of the research-cycle she fell into. First attempt: Google the item, compare listings, pick a number that "felt right." Half the time sat with no offers, half sold in an hour. Second attempt: eBay sold listings filtered by sold, last 90 days. Worked better. Sometimes. Until she realized sold listings weren't her exact item. Third attempt: Reddit r/whatsthisworth. Two days for a response. Three opinions ranging $10 to $200. Fourth attempt: WorthPoint subscription. Interface built for professionals. Cancelled after a week. $30 to confirm she's not a professional reseller.
**What they did:** Failed-solution stack with specific named alternatives (Google, eBay sold listings, Reddit subreddit name, WorthPoint), specific time durations, specific dollar amount on WorthPoint, and specific failure mode for each attempt.
**Why it works:** This is the failed-solution stack pattern executed in a non-health vertical. Four named attempts in a non-supplement context, each with specificity. The reader pattern-matches her own research cycles.
**Map:** Failed-solution stack section. Named alternatives with named platforms and dollar amounts.

### Beat 4: The Regret Cycle Escalation

**What they wrote:** "The worst part wasn't the time, though yeah, all those Saturdays add up. The worst part was the regret cycle." Then the three-line cycle: "List something → it sells fast → 'I priced too low again.' List something → it sits forever → 'I priced too high.' Donate the rest → 'what if some of that was actually worth real money?'"
**What they did:** Emotional escalation from financial loss to identity-undermining regret. The three-line cycle compresses the entire avatar experience into 30 words.
**Why it works:** The regret cycle is the equivalent of the consequences section in an advertorial. It's not "you'll lose money," it's "you're trapped in a loop that's slowly making you worse at this." The identity threat is real even though the stakes are financial rather than physical.
**Map:** Consequences section with identity-threat escalation. Pattern 7 (Five Core Feelings) "identity" feeling activated.

### Beat 5: The Breaking Point

**What they wrote:** Sunday night. Just sold a North Face jacket on Mercari for $22. Buyer wrote back: "thanks, I've been hunting for one of these - they go for $80 on Poshmark." The buyer wasn't gloating. She was thanking the protagonist. "That somehow made it worse. I just sat there. Not crying. Not angry. Just empty."
**What they did:** Specific breaking-point scene with a verbatim message from the buyer ("thanks, they go for $80 on Poshmark"). The buyer's gratitude is what makes it worse - the protagonist is being thanked for being exploited.
**Why it works:** The buyer's verbatim quote is the structural equivalent of Bev's quote in Roan ("I haven't slept in three years"). It externalizes the protagonist's failure through a specific named interaction.
**Map:** Decision moment. Named-patterns.md pattern 3 (Identification Before Mechanism) breaking-point scene with relational anchor.

### Beat 6: The Search That Changes Everything

**What they wrote:** Opened laptop. Searched something she hadn't searched before: "Why do I always price my stuff wrong." Not "best pricing app." Not "what's my item worth." Wanted to understand the WHY. Third result down: long post by a former eBay power seller who'd left the business.
**What they did:** The search-query itself is the discovery anchor. The shift from "best pricing app" to "why do I always price my stuff wrong" is the protagonist's recognition that the problem is conceptual, not tool-shaped.
**Why it works:** The discovery is mediated through a specific search query, which is the digital-context equivalent of the napkin in Roan or the patient conversation in PiPi Tea. The reader can imagine doing the same search.
**Map:** Mechanism reveal opener via search-anchored discovery. Named-patterns.md pattern 2 (Discovery Story Format) with search-query trigger variant.

### Beat 7: The Two-Layer Information Asymmetry Reveal

**What they wrote:** The eBay power seller's post explained that there are two layers of price data on the internet. Top layer: asking prices. Listing prices. What hopeful sellers want for their stuff. Almost meaningless. Anyone can list anything at any price. Bottom layer: sold-price data. Actual completed transactions. What real buyers, with real money, actually paid. Professional resellers have been using the bottom layer for years through subscription tools (WorthPoint, Terapeak) that cost $30-40/month and were never designed for casual sellers. Every casual seller has been working from the top layer. "That's why 'research' never worked for me. I wasn't researching. I was guessing using slightly better-organized guesses."
**What they did:** Pattern 4 (Three-Stage Mechanism Explanation) executed with information asymmetry as the mechanism. Stage 1 names the two layers. Stage 2 explains why they exist (subscription tools for pros). Stage 3 differentiates with specifics (named tools with prices, professional vs casual access). The "mechanism" is paywalled-knowledge structure.
**Why it works:** The information-asymmetry mechanism is fundamentally different from a body-mechanism but executes identically. Three stages: name, explain, differentiate. The named tools (WorthPoint $30-40/month) create a verifiable claim surface.
**Map:** Mechanism reveal section. Named-patterns.md pattern 4 (Three-Stage Mechanism Explanation) all three stages, with information-asymmetry mechanism rather than body-mechanism.

### Beat 8: The Frustration Amplification

**What they wrote:** "And here's what made me want to throw my laptop across the room. eBay's sold listings - the closest thing most of us have to real data - get harder to filter every year. Mercari makes historical sold prices nearly impossible to dig out. Facebook Marketplace shows you nothing. Poshmark shows you nothing. The data exists. Millions of completed transactions. You just don't have keys to the room."
**What they did:** Concentration on specific platforms (eBay, Mercari, Facebook Marketplace, Poshmark), each with specific limitation. The "keys to the room" metaphor externalizes the villain - the data exists, the protagonist is locked out.
**Why it works:** Pattern 4 concentration variant - the protagonist demolishes specific named platforms one by one. Each platform's limitation is mechanistic (filter limits, dig-out difficulty, no display). The aggregate effect is the information-asymmetry villain externalized to specific platform behaviors.
**Map:** Alternative attack section, executed through platform-by-platform concentration.

### Beat 9: Product Introduction (Pattern 4 Stage 4 - Access Differentiation)

**What they wrote:** The blog post ended with a recommendation. A tool the writer noticed that pulled actual sold-price data from eBay, Mercari, Poshmark and put it inside an app built for someone who is NOT a reseller. "Stop guessing. Look at what buyers actually paid. Last week, last month, last quarter." Protagonist takes a free 30-second quiz on the link mentioned. Gets matched to the app. Downloaded Thursday night. 30-day money-back guarantee.
**What they did:** Product reveal at approximately 60% of body word count - earlier than Derila or Roan, similar to PiPi Tea. The product differentiation is access-based: the same data professional resellers have, packaged for someone who is NOT a reseller. This is Pattern 4 Stage 4 (Preparation Differentiation) applied to information access rather than physical preparation.
**Why it works:** The product introduction is anticlimactic - the protagonist downloads it on a "what's there to lose" basis. The 30-day guarantee is mentioned in passing. This matches the by-the-way energy from the Derila and Roan ads.
**Map:** Product introduction section. Pattern 4 Stage 4 (Preparation Differentiation) applied to information-access context.

### Beat 10: Time-Anchored Results

**What they wrote:** Le Creuset Dutch oven scanned in three seconds: $185 based on actual completed sales. Vintage Levi's jacket: $78. Camera lenses: $125. Thirty-two items in about an hour. Week one: listed seven items at the actual sold prices, five sold within the week. Week two: nine more. Stopped second-guessing. Week three: sold a Coach bag for $145 that she would have priced at $40. By week six: cleared over $1,140 from stuff she'd been ready to donate or undersell for months. "Results obviously depend on what you actually have sitting around - but the point is I finally knew what was worth listing and what wasn't."
**What they did:** Specific time anchors (week one, two, three, six). Specific dollar amounts on specific items. Specific quantity metric (32 items in an hour). The qualifier "results obviously depend on what you actually have sitting around" preempts the reader's "but this is your situation, not mine" objection.
**Why it works:** Pattern 6 (Time-Delay Anchoring) executed across multiple weeks with multiple verifiable metrics. The Coach bag specifically (priced at $40 in her head, sold for $145) is the equivalent of the eGFR 42→54 metric in PiPi Tea - a verifiable before-and-after.
**Map:** Results timeline. Named-patterns.md pattern 6 (Time-Delay Anchoring) with mid-narrative qualifier.

### Beat 11: Network Validation + Close

**What they wrote:** Protagonist's sister noticed: "I don't understand. You used to spend two hours on one listing." Protagonist shows her the post about the two layers of pricing data. Sister reads it slowly, same face the protagonist had three weeks earlier. "I had no idea. I've been donating everything because I was sure I'd price it wrong." Close: four months later, no more 30-minute Google searches, no more donating in confusion. "I stopped pricing too low. I stopped pricing too high. I stopped guessing entirely. Not 'managed.' Not 'got better at it.' Actually stopped." Then explicit close: take the same free 30-second quiz the protagonist took. "Your gut feel isn't wrong. Your 'research' isn't lazy. You've just been working from the wrong layer of data this whole time."
**What they did:** Network validation through sister, then close with explicit address to the reader's interior monologue ("Your gut feel isn't wrong. Your 'research' isn't lazy"). The close reframes the reader's experience as systemic-injustice rather than personal-failure.
**Why it works:** The "your X isn't Y" reframe is the equivalent of Jean-Claude's "in Europe we consider it malpractice" in Roan - it externalizes the reader's experience to systemic forces. The close is by-the-way energy ("take the same free quiz I took"), not aggressive CTA.
**Map:** Durability proof + Mirror moment + Close, merged. Named-patterns.md pattern 7 (Five Core Feelings) "vindication" feeling closed, with reframe-as-systemic-injustice.

### Ten key learnings from the ValueScout breakdown

1. The long-form-static framework is portable to non-health verticals when the surface elements are translated (information asymmetry as villain instead of body-mechanism).
2. First-person sufferer POV works in non-physical-suffering contexts (financial loss + regret cycle is sufficient stakes).
3. Pattern 4 Stage 4 (Preparation Differentiation) applies to information-access context (the same data, packaged for the wrong audience) just as it applies to physical preparation.
4. Contrarian-counsel hooks ("please stop doing X") are a variant of Pattern 1 (Desperation Frame Opener) that work when the reader's reflex is to defend the current behavior.
5. Failed-solution stacks in non-health verticals use specific named platforms / tools / approaches the same way health stacks use specific named supplements.
6. The buyer-quote breaking-point ("thanks, they go for $80 on Poshmark") is the relational anchor equivalent of Bev's quote in Roan - specific external voice that crystallizes the failure.
7. Pattern 4 mechanism explanation works with information-asymmetry as the mechanism content (two-layer data structure) just as it works with body-mechanism content.
8. Concentration on specific platforms (eBay, Mercari, Facebook Marketplace, Poshmark) is the digital equivalent of concentrating on specific competitor supplements in a health ad.
9. Verifiable specific outcomes (Coach bag priced at $40 vs. sold $145) carry the same weight as clinical metrics in health ads.
10. The "your gut isn't wrong, your research isn't lazy" reframe externalizes the reader's failure to systemic forces, which is the non-medical equivalent of "your body isn't broken, the mechanism is."

## Cross-example patterns

What 3 or 4 of the 4 examples do (universal patterns across POV, vertical, authority type):

**Late product reveal.** Derila ~77%, Roan ~73%, PiPi Tea ~65%, ValueScout ~60%. The product reveal lands later in narrative-mechanism-led ads (Derila, Roan) and earlier in preparation-differentiation or access-differentiation ads (PiPi Tea, ValueScout). Range: 60-80%. The rule is "after the mechanism has been earned," not a fixed percentage.

**Specific named alternatives with dollar amounts and durations on failed attempts.** Derila (CPAP $800, mouthguard $400, smart tracker $200). Roan ($3,200 over seven years on six named treatments). PiPi Tea (cranberry supplements, kidney support blends, cutting protein - shorter stack because narrator credibility is high). ValueScout (Google, eBay sold listings, Reddit, WorthPoint $30/month). Every example names specific alternatives with verifiable specifics.

**Relational stakes beyond the individual.** Derila (daughter watching father). Roan (Bev's three years of not sleeping). PiPi Tea (Sandra's 38 years of nursing, 5 patients she's recommending to). ValueScout (the sister's "I've been donating everything"). Every example externalizes the protagonist's experience to a named relationship.

**Modest specific results, not dramatic transformations.** Derila (snoring stopped day 4). Roan (3 trips to 1, some nights zero). PiPi Tea (eGFR 42 → 58 in three months). ValueScout ($1,140 over six weeks). Specific verifiable outcomes, not "life-changing" or "miraculous" framing.

**Mechanism delivered through dialogue, observation, internal research, or referenced content** - varies by lead POV:
- Derila: dialogue with informal authority (Henrik)
- Roan: dialogue with formal credentialed authority (Jean-Claude)
- PiPi Tea: observed outcome trigger + internal research (Sandra reads up on hibiscus antioxidants)
- ValueScout: referenced content trigger (a blog post by a former eBay power seller)

All four avoid pure exposition. The mechanism arrives mediated through a specific surface (named character, observed outcome, named blog post, internal research with named compounds). The surface varies; the principle is constant.

**Story-as-camouflage all the way through.** All 4 examples read as personal stories end-to-end. The brand surfaces only at the product reveal. No "introducing our product" framing; no marketing language at the reveal.

**Hook intensity calibrated to vertical.** Death-stakes openers (ICU patient died, $3,200 lost over 7 years, 38-year nurse with stage 3 kidney disease) are appropriate for high-stakes verticals. Contrarian-counsel openers ("please stop trying to price your own stuff") work for lower-stakes verticals where the avatar's reflex is defensive. The framework adjusts to vertical without changing structural shape.

## What all 4 examples avoid

- Marketing language at the product reveal.
- Aggressive tone toward alternatives. CPAP, statins, capsule Saw Palmetto, generic hibiscus tea, and underpriced selling platforms are described as "what didn't work for me" or "what's making this harder than it needs to be" - not as "the broken industry's failed solutions."
- Stating the mechanism via exposition. The mechanism is always mediated through a surface (dialogue, observation, research, referenced content).
- Overclaiming. Results are specific and modest. No "miracle," no "life-changing," no "transformed everything."

## Compliance Gate

Run this gate BEFORE using any worked example beat as a template. This applies to ALL four examples (Derila, Roan, PiPi Tea, ValueScout), not just the example with the explicit compliance redline.

Worked examples teach structural patterns that have produced high-converting copy in real campaigns. The structural patterns are reusable. The surface elements (specific outcomes, named conditions, named brands, dollar amounts, dose specifics, regulatory references) are NOT automatically reusable - they are the example's actual surface, which may or may not be compliant for the brand the operator is writing for.

Before treating any beat as a template, verify:

- [ ] **Vertical compliance.** Does the example's vertical (supplement / reselling / etc.) have specific regulatory or platform constraints on the claims being made? For supplement and health verticals, structure-function claim language (FDA), substantiation requirements (FTC), and platform review (Meta, TikTok) all apply. For financial or investment verticals, FINRA / SEC frameworks apply. For reselling / arbitrage, FTC endorsement guides apply.
- [ ] **Specific clinical / financial / outcome metrics.** Does the example reference specific clinical-test results, specific financial return figures, or specific outcomes ("eGFR 42 → 58," "$1,140 over six weeks," "blood pressure 154 → 122") that the operator's brand cannot substantiate at the same level? If so, replace with qualitative descriptions or general benefit language compliant with the operator's substantiation evidence.
- [ ] **Named diagnosed conditions.** Does the example name a diagnosed medical condition the protagonist has ("Stage 3 kidney disease," "Type 2 diabetes," "BPH at 58")? Supplement copy generally must NOT claim to treat, cure, or stabilize diagnosed conditions. Reframe to structure-function language for the brand's copy.
- [ ] **Standard-of-care critique.** Does the example critique or position the product as an alternative to standard medical treatment for a diagnosed condition (dialysis, prescription medication, surgery)? For supplement and health verticals, be extremely cautious - this can trigger both regulatory and platform consequences. The protagonist's "I refused dialysis" or "I poured the finasteride into the trash" framing must NOT be reproduced without explicit legal review.
- [ ] **Patient-network or aggregated outcome claims.** Does the example aggregate clinical outcomes across multiple subjects ("five of my patients all reported stable function," "every patient I've recommended this to")? Aggregated outcome claims require clinical substantiation most brands cannot provide. Reframe as individual experience, not aggregated evidence.
- [ ] **Named competitor brands or pharmaceutical names.** Does the example name specific competitor products, brand names, or pharmaceuticals (Flomax, Finasteride, Lisinopril, specific Saw Palmetto brands, Permixon, Tadenan)? Naming competitors can trigger trademark issues; naming pharmaceuticals can trigger prescription-drug advertising rules. The operator's brand must verify what naming is acceptable for its compliance posture.
- [ ] **Foreign pharmacology references.** Does the example reference foreign medical systems or formulations (European pharmacy, French pharmacist authority, Permixon, ortie racine)? Be aware that "available in Europe but not in America" framing implies pharmaceutical-grade efficacy that supplement claims cannot legally support in the US market.

When ANY check identifies a non-reusable surface element, the structural pattern can still be adapted - the operator must replace the non-compliant surface with brand-compliant surface that preserves the structural function. For example: replace "eGFR 42 → 58" (clinical metric) with "I noticed I had more energy by week 6" (qualitative outcome) while preserving the time-anchored result pattern.

## Audit Checklist

Run your draft against this checklist. This is INCREMENTAL to the 14-point self-test in `self-test-checklist.md`, not a replacement. The 14-point test still runs first; this checklist runs after, focused on the beats that worked examples have validated.

- **Hook intensity.** Calibrated to vertical: death-stakes for high-stakes verticals, contrarian-counsel or financial-loss for lower-stakes. Specific enough to pass the time/identity/specificity test in the first 9 words?
- **Worst-moment opening.** Dropped into the moment, or built up to it?
- **Mechanism delivery surface.** Dialogue with authority, observed outcome, self-research as authority, or referenced content - not pure exposition?
- **Failed attempts specificity.** Exact dollar amounts, named alternatives, durations, in the avatar's voice?
- **Pattern 4 Stage 4 (Preparation Differentiation).** When the mechanism class is widely available, does the differentiation move to preparation/source/format/access specifics?
- **Late reveal.** Product name at or after 60% mark? (Range: 60-80%; later for mechanism-led ads, earlier for preparation-led ads.)
- **Specific verifiable results.** Outcomes with metrics the reader could verify (clinical scores, dollar amounts, specific quantities), not feel-based language?
- **Modest result framing.** Results stated specifically without "miracle" / "life-changing" / "transformation" language?
- **Relational anchor.** A specific named relationship that externalizes stakes (family, spouse, patient network, sister, friend, etc.)?
- **Mirror in After.** Every identification-opener wound healed in the results timeline?
- **Close tone.** "By the way" energy, or "BUY NOW" energy?
